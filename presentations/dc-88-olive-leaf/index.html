Kirtland, Ohio · December 27–28, 1832 & January 3, 1833

The Olive Leaf

“The Lord’s message of peace to us”
Doctrine and Covenants 88
141 verses · A deep study
Welcome. Today we study Doctrine and Covenants section 88 — the longest section in the Doctrine and Covenants, and the one Joseph Smith named "the olive leaf … plucked from the Tree of Paradise, the Lord's message of peace to us." It was received over three sittings — December 27 and 28, 1832, and January 3, 1833 — at a conference of high priests in Kirtland. Fair warning: this section is enormous, and it moves from the immensity of space to what time you should go to bed. We are going to trace that movement, because the movement itself is the message. Press 'S' for speaker notes. Arrow keys to navigate.
The name

Why “the Olive Leaf”?

Three days earlier the Lord had given Section 87 — the prophecy on war, forecasting war “poured out upon all nations.” Section 88 is the deliberate answer to it.
“And the dove came in to him in the evening; and, lo, in her mouth was an olive leaf pluckt off: so Noah knew that the waters were abated from off the earth.”
— Genesis 8:11
The leaf is not the end of the flood. It is evidence that something is alive inside it.
The title is the interpretive key to the whole section, so it's worth slowing down here. Section 87 — three days earlier — is the prophecy on war. It names South Carolina. It forecasts war poured out on all nations. Section 88 follows immediately, and Joseph calls it the olive leaf. Genesis 8: Noah sends out the dove, and it comes back with an olive leaf. That leaf did not mean the flood was over — the ark was still floating. It meant that somewhere out there, something was alive, and growing. Hold onto that, because in about ten slides we're going to hit earthquakes, a moon bathed in blood, and seven angels with trumpets — inside the "message of peace." That is not a contradiction. That's the point.

Five words carry the section: light · law · kingdom · all things · house.
That word list is the skeleton of the doctrine.
A quick orientation by the numbers. 141 verses — the longest section in the Doctrine and Covenants, about 4,400 words. "Light" appears 25 times. "Kingdom" or "kingdoms," 28. "Law," 20. The phrase "all things," 24 times — and eight of those are in verse 41 alone. "House" appears 18 times, and eight of those are in verse 119 alone. This isn't trivia. When a text repeats a word this hard, it's telling you what it's about. Light, law, kingdom, all things, house. If you remember nothing else from tonight, those five words will get you back into the section.

The section travels from “the immensity of space” (v. 12) to what time to go to bed (v. 124) — and the descent is not a change of subject.
Here's the map. Five movements. One through five: the promise of another Comforter. Six through fifty: the great cosmological passage — light, law, kingdoms. Fifty-one to sixty-one: a parable found nowhere else in scripture. Sixty-two through one-sixteen: the commandments to seek, sanctify, and teach, plus the apocalypse. And one-seventeen to the end: build a house. Now note the line at the bottom, because it's the structural key. Verse 12 talks about light filling the immensity of space. Verse 124 tells you to go to bed early and get up early. That is an enormous drop in scale — and it is not the Lord changing the subject. It's the same principle applied at both ends. One law governs planetary orbits and sleep schedules, and both are sanctified the same way: by abiding it.
Movement I · Verses 1–5

Another Comforter

“The alms of your prayers have come up into the ears of the Lord of Sabaoth, and are recorded in the book of the names of the sanctified…”
— D&C 88:2

The gift

“Another Comforter… the Holy Spirit of promise” — the same promised in John's testimony.

The content

“The promise which I give unto you of eternal life, even the glory of the celestial kingdom.”

The section opens with a compliment, which is rare. "This is pleasing unto your Lord, and the angels rejoice over you." Then that arresting phrase: "the alms of your prayers." Alms are charitable offerings. Their prayers are being described as a gift they gave — not only as requests they made. Worth sitting with: is your prayer an ask, or an offering? And then the gift itself. Verse 3: "another Comforter, even the Holy Spirit of promise." Verse 4 defines it — "the promise which I give unto you of eternal life." This connects to John 14 and to the doctrine of the Second Comforter. The whole section will end up circling back to this: verse 68's promise that "he will unveil his face unto you."
Movement II · Verses 6–10

He Descended, Therefore He Comprehends

“He that ascended up on high, as also he descended below all things, in that he comprehended all things, that he might be in all and through all things, the light of truth.”
— D&C 88:6

The sun

“He is in the sun, and the light of the sun, and the power thereof by which it was made.”

The moon & stars

The same light, and the same power by which they were made.

Your mind

“The same light that quickeneth your understandings.”

Now the great passage. Verse 6 makes a causal claim that is easy to read past: he descended below all things — "in that" — meaning *by that means* — he comprehended all things. Descent is the precondition of comprehension. He didn't understand suffering from above it. He went underneath it. That's Alma 7:11-12 stated cosmologically. Then verses 7 through 11 do something almost no other scripture does. The light of Christ is the light of the sun — and the power by which the sun was made. And the moon. And the stars. And the earth. And then, without any change of gear, "the same light that quickeneth your understandings." The light that makes the stars burn is the light that makes you able to think. Same light. That's not poetry in this section — it's a claim about what the universe is made of.
The hinge of the section

Verse 13

“The light which is in all things, which giveth life to all things, which is the law by which all things are governed, even the power of God who sitteth upon his throne…”
Physics= Biology= Law= Mind= A Person
If you take one verse home tonight, take this one. In a single sentence, light is identified with five things at once. What makes stars burn — physics. What gives life to all things — biology. The law by which all things are governed — jurisprudence. What quickens understanding, from the verse before — cognition. And finally, "even the power of God who sitteth upon his throne" — a Person. Most of the Western tradition partitions these. Natural law over here; moral law over there. Science in one building, religion in another. Verse 13 refuses the partition. It says they are one substance, and that substance is a Person. That is why this section can move from planetary orbits to your bedtime without flinching. It's all one law.
Verses 14–16

The Spirit and the Body Are the Soul

“And the spirit and the body are the soul of man. And the resurrection from the dead is the redemption of the soul.”
— D&C 88:15–16

What it rejects

The body as the soul’s prison. A disembodied spirit is not a freed soul — it is an incomplete one.

What it requires

Salvation is embodied. Resurrection is not an appendix to redemption; it is redemption.

Eight words that undo about two thousand years of philosophy: "the spirit and the body are the soul of man." In the Platonic tradition, and in most creedal Christianity downstream of it, the body is what the soul is trying to escape. Death is the great liberation. Not here. Here the body is half the soul. Which means death doesn't free you — it dismembers you. And that reframes resurrection completely. Verse 16: "the resurrection from the dead is the redemption of the soul." Resurrection isn't a nice bonus at the end of salvation. It *is* salvation, finished. This is why we care about bodies. Why the Word of Wisdom matters. Why we get up early, per verse 124.
Verses 17–20, 25–26

The Earth Keeps Its Covenant

“The earth abideth the law of a celestial kingdom, for it filleth the measure of its creation, and transgresseth not the law.”
— D&C 88:25
Dies→ Quickened→ Sanctified→ Inherited by the meek
The earth is held up as the one creation already doing what the Saints are being asked to do.
Here's a passage we tend to skip. The earth is described as a covenant-keeping being. It abides the law of a celestial kingdom. It doesn't transgress. And so it will die, be quickened, be sanctified, and be inherited by — verse 17 — "the poor and the meek." The rhetorical function is comparison. In this whole passage, the earth is the one thing that's already obedient. It's being held up in front of the Saints. And notice the standard it meets, because it's a phrase worth memorizing: it "filleth the measure of its creation." Not more than it was made for. Not less. The exact measure. That's a mercifully specific definition of a well-lived life. You are not asked to be someone else's measure. You are asked to fill your own.
Verses 21–24, 40

Judgment as Capacity, Not Verdict

“He who is not able to abide the law of a celestial kingdom cannot abide a celestial glory.”
— D&C 88:22
The verb is abide — to remain, to endure, to stay. The grammar is diagnostic, not punitive. Nobody is expelled from celestial glory; some are unable to remain in it.
“Intelligence cleaveth unto intelligence; wisdom receiveth wisdom; truth embraceth truth; virtue loveth virtue; light cleaveth unto light…”
— D&C 88:40
Verses 22 through 24 repeat one verb three times: abide. He who is not *able to abide* the law cannot abide the glory. That's the language of capacity, not sentencing. It's closer to a physician's diagnosis than a judge's ruling. Nobody gets thrown out of the celestial kingdom in this passage — some people simply couldn't stay there. And verse 40 gives the mechanism: the law of affinity. Intelligence cleaves to intelligence. Light cleaves to light. Like gathers to like, by nature. Which means the sorting at the last day is largely self-sorting. You will end up where you are most at home. That should be both comforting and terrifying, and it makes one question urgent right now: what am I currently cleaving to?
Verses 32–33

The Gift Refused

“They shall return again to their own place, to enjoy that which they are willing to receive, because they were not willing to enjoy that which they might have received.”
— D&C 88:32
“For what doth it profit a man if a gift is bestowed upon him, and he receive not the gift? Behold, he rejoices not in that which is given unto him, neither rejoices in him who is the giver of the gift.”
— D&C 88:33
This is, to me, the most merciful and the most sobering passage in the section, and it's two verses long. Verse 32: they return to their own place, to enjoy what they *are willing* to receive — because they were not willing to enjoy what they *might have* received. Read that twice. The limiting factor is not the offer. The limiting factor is willingness. Then verse 33, which reads almost like a sigh: what does it profit a man if a gift is bestowed on him and he doesn't receive it? And then the second half, which is the part that gets me. He doesn't rejoice in the gift — "neither rejoices in him who is the giver of the gift." The real loss isn't the unopened box. It's the unknown Giver. You can be the recipient of everything God has and still not know Him, because you never opened anything He sent. Practical question for the class: what has already been given to you that you haven't opened yet?
Verses 34–38

Law Is the Soil, Not the Fence

“That which is governed by law is also preserved by law and perfected and sanctified by the same.”
— D&C 88:34

No lawless space

“There is no space in the which there is no kingdom; and there is no kingdom in which there is no space.” (v. 37)

A law unto itself

What “seeketh to become a law unto itself… cannot be sanctified by law, neither by mercy, justice, nor judgment.” (v. 35)

We usually think of law as the fence around the field. This section says law is the soil the field is made of. Verse 34: what is governed by law is preserved by law, and perfected, and sanctified by the same law. Law isn't what limits growth — it's the mechanism of growth. Verse 37 pushes it as far as it will go: there is no space without a kingdom, and no kingdom without space. There is no lawless territory anywhere in the universe. Not one cubic inch. Which makes verse 35 devastating. Whatever "seeketh to become a law unto itself" cannot be sanctified — and look at the list — not by law, not by justice, not by judgment, and *not by mercy*. Mercy is on that list. Not because God withholds it, but because a being that has stepped outside law has stepped outside the only mechanism by which sanctification operates. It's not that the door is locked. It's that they've walked out of the building.
Verse 41 · “all things” eight times in one sentence

The Encircling God

“He comprehendeth all things, and all things are before him, and all things are round about him; and he is above all things, and in all things, and is through all things, and is round about all things; and all things are by him, and of him, even God, forever and ever.”
The syntax performs the doctrine — the sentence encircles you the way God encircles all things.
Read this one out loud. It's the rhetorical high point of the section. Eight times: "all things." And count the prepositions — before him, round about him, above, in, through, round about again, by him, of him. The sentence doesn't just *describe* being surrounded. It *does* it. By the time you finish reading it you've been circled seven or eight times. That's deliberate. The syntax performs the doctrine. And it lands on a word that matters after everything we've just said about law: "even God." Not a force. Not a field. A Him.
The section’s most loaded verb

Two Meanings of “Comprehend”

To encompass

v. 6 — He “descended below all things, in that he comprehended all things.”
v. 43 — the courses of the heavens “comprehend the earth and all the planets.”

To understand

v. 49 — “The light shineth in darkness, and the darkness comprehendeth it not.”
v. 49 — “The day shall come when you shall comprehend even God.”

Then verse 67 collapses both: “that body which is filled with light comprehendeth all things.”
Christ comprehends by descending. We comprehend by being filled.
The word "comprehend" shows up seven times in six verses, and the section plays its two meanings against each other on purpose. The old meaning is physical: to encompass, to contain. That's verse 6 — he comprehended all things by descending below them — and verse 43, where planetary orbits "comprehend" the planets. The modern meaning is mental: to understand. That's verse 49, quoting John 1 — the darkness comprehended it not. And then verse 67 fuses them. "That body which is filled with light comprehendeth all things." The body filled with light both *contains* and *understands*. Here's the doctrine that falls out of the wordplay: Christ comprehends by descending. We comprehend by being filled. Neither one is primarily an act of intellect. Comprehension is a capacity produced by saturation — and the saturation is the part we control.
Movement III · Verses 51–61

The Parable of the Twelve Kingdoms

A lord sends twelve servants into his field. He visits the first in the first hour, the second in the second — on to the twelfth — and tarries with each the full hour. Found nowhere else in scripture.
“And thus they all received the light of the countenance of their lord, every man in his hour, and in his time, and in his season.”
— D&C 88:58

Cosmological

Worlds without number, each in its season (v. 61).

Historical

The sequence of dispensations.

Pastoral

God’s attention is not divided — it is sequential and total.

This parable is unique to the Restoration. There's nothing like it in the Bible. The setup is simple. Twelve servants, one field. The lord comes to the first in the first hour and — this is the detail that matters — "tarried with him all that hour." Then the second, then the third, on to the twelfth. Three readings, and all three are in the text. Verse 61 gives the cosmological one explicitly: "unto this parable I will liken all these kingdoms" — worlds without number. The historical one: dispensations, each in its season. But the pastoral reading is the one that will help someone in your class on Tuesday. God's attention is not divided among his children — it's sequential, and it's total. When the lord withdraws from the first servant, that is not abandonment. It's the same undivided attention, moving. And verse 59 shuts the door on despair about your place in the line: "beginning at the first, and so on unto the last, and from the last unto the first, and from the first unto the last." The circuit runs both directions. No last is finally last. If it is not your hour yet — it is coming.
Movement IV · Verses 62–65

Draw Near

“Draw near unto me and I will draw near unto you; seek me diligently and ye shall find me; ask, and ye shall receive; knock, and it shall be opened unto you.”
— D&C 88:63
And the counterweight most treatments omit: “If ye ask anything that is not expedient for you, it shall turn unto your condemnation.”
— D&C 88:65
Verse 63 is the one everybody knows, and it deserves its fame. Four conditionals stacked: draw near, seek, ask, knock. Every one of them puts the first move on us. Note that this is not God being coy. Verse 62 says "call upon me *while I am near*" — He is already close. The distance is on our side of the equation. But then verse 65, which almost never gets quoted, and I think it should. "If ye ask anything that is not expedient for you, it shall turn unto your condemnation." Unanswered prayer is not the only risk in prayer. Answered prayer can be. Sometimes the most merciful thing God does is decline. This verse is the reason "thy will be done" is not a formality at the end of a prayer — it's the safety mechanism.
Verses 67–69 · The great and last promise

If Your Eye Be Single

“And if your eye be single to my glory, your whole bodies shall be filled with light, and there shall be no darkness in you; and that body which is filled with light comprehendeth all things.”
— D&C 88:67
“Sanctify yourselves that your minds become single to God, and the days will come that you shall see him; for he will unveil his face unto you… in his own time, and in his own way, and according to his own will.”
— D&C 88:68
Now the section closes the loop it opened in verse 12. There, light filled the immensity of space. Here, light fills a body. The cosmic aperture narrows to a personal one. "Single" here means undivided — one focus, not one glance. And notice the mechanism: singleness isn't the reward for being full of light, it's the *aperture* that lets the light in. Divided attention is a partly-closed shutter. Verse 68 is what the whole section has been driving toward: the great and last promise. "The days will come that you shall see him; for he will unveil his face unto you." And then the three qualifiers, which are pastorally essential: in his own time, in his own way, according to his own will. Not on demand. Not on your calendar. But promised. For the Kirtland Saints this was not abstract — three and a half years later, in the Kirtland Temple, it happened. Sections 109 and 110.
Verses 76–80, 118

Teach Ye Diligently

“Teach ye diligently and my grace shall attend you… of things both in heaven and in the earth, and under the earth; things which have been, things which are, things which must shortly come to pass… the wars and the perplexities of the nations… and a knowledge also of countries and of kingdoms.”
— D&C 88:78–79
Astronomy·Geology·History·Current events·Geopolitics·Geography
“And as all have not faith, seek ye diligently and teach one another words of wisdom; yea, seek ye out of the best books words of wisdom; seek learning, even by study and also by faith.”
— D&C 88:118
Two things here that we tend to underread. First, verse 78: "Teach ye diligently and my grace shall attend you." Grace is promised to attend an act of teaching. If you have a calling that involves preparing lessons, that promise is addressed to you by name. Second, look at the syllabus in verse 79. Things in heaven, in the earth, under the earth. Things past, present, and shortly to come. Things at home and abroad. The wars and perplexities of the nations. A knowledge of countries and kingdoms. That's astronomy, geology, history, current events, geopolitics, and geography — assigned by revelation in 1832. And the stated motive in verse 80 is not enrichment. It's readiness: "that ye may be prepared in all things when I shall send you again." Then verse 118 gives the method, and don't skip the first clause: "*and as all have not faith* — seek ye out of the best books." Study is introduced as the accommodation for a community whose faith is uneven. It's not the lesser path. It's the door that is always open, even on the days faith is thin.
Verses 87–116 · The apocalyptic center

Seven Angels, Seven Trumps

Then they sound a second time (vv. 108–110), each revealing “the secret acts of men, and the thoughts and intents of their hearts, and the mighty works of God” for its thousand years.
This is the material that sits, surprisingly, inside a document called the olive leaf. Earthquakes. The moon bathed in blood. Men's hearts failing them. Seven angels with seven trumpets. The trumps map onto the order of the resurrection — first the celestial, "Christ's, the first fruits"; second those who accepted the gospel in spirit prison; third those under condemnation, who wait out the millennium; fourth those who remain filthy still. Then the fifth carries the everlasting gospel to all nations, the sixth announces the fall of Babylon, and the seventh: "It is finished." Now the detail I find most striking. In verses 108 through 110, the trumps sound *again*. And this time each one reveals the secret acts of men, the thoughts and intents of their hearts, and the mighty works of God — for its thousand years. In order. All of it. That is a remarkable model of final judgment. It isn't primarily a sentencing. It's a *disclosure*. History read back aloud, in full, with nothing left out — including, note, "the mighty works of God," which most of us have never seen. And verse 88 sets the order, which is not the one we expect: "after your testimony cometh wrath." Testimony comes first. Which is why verse 81 says every man who has been warned is to warn his neighbor. The warning is the mercy.
Movement V · Verse 119

Establish a House

“Organize yourselves; prepare every needful thing; and establish a house…”
prayer· fasting· faith· learning· glory· order· God
Seven descriptors — and learning sits at the center, fourth of seven, flanked by faith and glory. Given 3½ years before the Kirtland Temple was dedicated.
Verse 119. Eight uses of the word "house" in a single verse. A house of prayer, of fasting, of faith, of learning, of glory, of order, of God. Seven descriptors — and I want you to notice where learning falls. Fourth of seven. Dead center, flanked by faith on one side and glory on the other. The house of God is a school. And the section makes that literal — verses 127 through 141 legislate the School of the Prophets: who stands where, how they greet each other, the ordinance of washing feet after the pattern of John 13. And the timing matters. This was given in January 1833. The Kirtland Temple wasn't dedicated until March 1836. Section 88 is the commission; Sections 109 and 110 are the fulfillment — including the appearance of the Savior, which is verse 68's great and last promise, kept.
Verses 121–126

From the Immensity of Space to Bedtime

“Cease to be idle; cease to be unclean; cease to find fault one with another; cease to sleep longer than is needful; retire to thy bed early, that ye may not be weary; arise early, that your bodies and your minds may be invigorated.”
— D&C 88:124

v. 122 — how a class runs

“Let not all be spokesmen at once; but let one speak at a time and let all listen… that all may be edified of all, and that every man may have an equal privilege.”

v. 125 — above all things

“Clothe yourselves with the bond of charity, as with a mantle, which is the bond of perfectness and peace.”

Here is the tonal whiplash, and it is the most important structural feature of the section. We have just come through the wine-press of the wrath of Almighty God. And the very next thing the Lord says is: go to bed at a reasonable hour and get up on time. That is not an anticlimax. It's the argument. The same law that fixes the courses of the planets governs when you go to bed — and the stated reason is beautifully practical: "that your bodies and your minds may be invigorated." Fit for the work. Two verses to take with you. Verse 122 is a complete charter for how a class should be run: one speaks at a time, everyone listens, everyone is edified by everyone, and every person has an *equal privilege*. Read that in your next teacher council. And verse 125 — "above all things." Not among other things. Above them. Clothe yourselves with charity as with a mantle. A mantle is an outer garment. It goes on over everything else, and it's the thing people see first.
Verses 127–141 · The School of the Prophets

Friendship as an Ordinance

“I salute you in the name of the Lord Jesus Christ… in a determination that is fixed, immovable, and unchangeable, to be your friend and brother through the grace of God in the bonds of love, to walk in all the commandments of God blameless, in thanksgiving, forever and ever.”
— D&C 88:133
The section closes with the washing of feet — “according to the pattern given in the thirteenth chapter of John’s testimony” (v. 141).
The last movement legislates the School of the Prophets, and one detail deserves attention. Verse 133 is the prescribed greeting between members of the school. And listen to what it actually is: a covenant of friendship. "In a determination that is fixed, immovable, and unchangeable, to be your friend and brother." Friendship is made a formal ordinance. Not a pleasantry — a covenant, with the same vocabulary the scriptures use for the everlasting covenant itself. And then the section ends by pointing back to John 13 — the washing of feet, at the Last Supper. So the School of the Prophets isn't framed as a curriculum. It's framed as discipleship, in the room where the Savior knelt down in front of his friends. That's how the longest revelation in the Doctrine and Covenants ends: with a basin and a towel.
The governing thesis

This Is the Olive Leaf

Section 88 answers Section 87’s prophecy of war not by denying the war, but by revealing what holds while it happens.
Light fills all space. Law governs every kingdom. And in the middle of the earthquakes and the trumps, the Saints are told to organize themselves, establish a house of learning and order and glory, teach one another, speak one at a time, go to bed early, and put on charity like a coat.
Not the announcement that the flood is over — the evidence that something is alive inside it, and the instructions for building on the ground as it dries.
Let's land it. Section 88 is the answer to Section 87. But notice *how* it answers. It does not say the war won't come. It says the war is not the deepest thing that's true. Light fills all space. Law governs every kingdom. Christ is in the sun and in your understanding. The earth keeps its covenant. Every servant gets his full hour. The gift has already been bestowed — the only question is whether it gets opened. And then, in the middle of the earthquakes and the trumps, the instructions are startlingly ordinary. Organize yourselves. Build a house of learning. Teach each other. Don't all talk at once. Go to bed. Get up. Put on charity like a coat. That's the olive leaf. Noah's dove didn't bring news that the flood was over — the ark was still floating. It brought a leaf: proof that somewhere, something was alive and growing, and that there would be ground to build on. Section 88 is that leaf. And the last thing it tells us to do is build. Bear testimony and close.
