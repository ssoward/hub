The Olive Leaf

“The Lord’s message of peace to us”
Doctrine and Covenants 88
141 verses · A deep study
Welcome. Today we study Doctrine and Covenants section 88 — the longest section in the Doctrine and Covenants, and the one Joseph Smith named "the olive leaf … plucked from the Tree of Paradise, the Lord's message of peace to us." DATES AND SETTING • Received across three sittings: verses 1–126 on December 27–28, 1832; verses 127–141 on January 3, 1833. • Kirtland, Ohio, at a conference of high priests. • Joseph's "olive leaf" description comes from his January 11, 1833 letter to W. W. Phelps in Independence, Missouri. SCALE • 141 verses — the longest section in the D&C. About 4,438 words. • It is the founding charter for two institutions: the Kirtland Temple (v. 119) and the School of the Prophets (vv. 127–141). WHY IT IS HARD TO TEACH The section covers cosmology, resurrection, degrees of glory, law, a parable, an apocalypse, and household habits. Most lessons pick one thread and drop the rest. Tonight we trace the movement from the immensity of space (v. 12) to bedtime (v. 124), because that movement is itself the message. Press 'S' for speaker notes. Arrow keys to navigate.
The name

Why “the Olive Leaf”?

Two days earlier — on Christmas Day, 1832 — the Lord had given Section 87, the prophecy on war, forecasting war “poured out upon all nations.” Section 88 is the deliberate answer to it.
“And the dove came in to him in the evening; and, lo, in her mouth was an olive leaf pluckt off: so Noah knew that the waters were abated from off the earth.”
— Genesis 8:11
The leaf is not the end of the flood. It is evidence that something is alive inside it.
The title is the interpretive key to the whole section, so it's worth slowing down here. CONTEXT • Section 87 — the prophecy on war — was given two days earlier, on Christmas Day 1832. It names South Carolina and forecasts war "poured out upon all nations." • Section 88 begins December 27. Joseph calls it the olive leaf. THE ALLUSION Genesis 8:11. Noah sends out the dove; it returns with an olive leaf. Note carefully what that leaf did and did not mean. It did not mean the flood was over — the ark was still floating, and it would be weeks before they left it. It meant that somewhere out there, above water, something was alive and growing. KEY TERM • Olive leaf — in the ancient Mediterranean the olive is the emblem of peace, and olive oil is the substance of anointing, healing, and light (it fuels the lamps of Matthew 25). All four senses are live in this section. THE PARADOX TO HOLD In about ten slides we hit earthquakes, a moon bathed in blood, and seven angels with trumpets — inside a document called "the Lord's message of peace." That is not a contradiction. The peace of section 88 is not the absence of catastrophe. It is light, law, and a house of God located inside it.

Movement I · Verses 1–5

Another Comforter

“The alms of your prayers have come up into the ears of the Lord of Sabaoth, and are recorded in the book of the names of the sanctified…”
— D&C 88:2

The gift

“Another Comforter… the Holy Spirit of promise” — the same promised in John's testimony.

The content

“The promise which I give unto you of eternal life, even the glory of the celestial kingdom.”

The section opens with a commendation, which is rare in the Doctrine and Covenants: "this is pleasing unto your Lord, and the angels rejoice over you." "THE ALMS OF YOUR PRAYERS" (v. 2) • Alms — charitable gifts to the poor (Matthew 6:1–4). The compound reframes prayer as something offered, not only something requested. • Lord of Sabaoth — from the Hebrew tsebaoth, "hosts" or "armies." D&C 95:7 glosses it directly: "the Lord of Sabaoth, which is by interpretation, the creator of the first day." • "The book of the names of the sanctified" — compare the book of life, Revelation 20:12 and D&C 128:7. THE GIFT (vv. 3–5) • "Another Comforter" — the phrase is from John 14:16. The First Comforter is the Holy Ghost; scripture and Joseph Smith's teaching identify a Second Comforter, the personal appearance of the Lord (John 14:18, 21, 23). • "Holy Spirit of promise" — the sealing and ratifying power that validates covenants and ordinances (D&C 132:7; Ephesians 1:13). • Verse 4 defines the gift as "the promise… of eternal life, even the glory of the celestial kingdom." WHERE THIS GOES Hold this thread. Verse 68's "great and last promise" — "he will unveil his face unto you" — is the same one, and it was kept in the Kirtland Temple in 1836 (D&C 110). DISCUSSION Is your prayer mostly an ask? Is any of it an offering?
Movement II · Verses 6–10

He Descended, Therefore He Comprehends

“He that ascended up on high, as also he descended below all things, in that he comprehended all things, that he might be in all and through all things, the light of truth.”
— D&C 88:6

The sun

“He is in the sun, and the light of the sun, and the power thereof by which it was made.”

The moon & stars

The same light, and the same power by which they were made.

Your mind

“The same light that quickeneth your understandings.”

Verse 6 makes a causal claim that is easy to read past. THE GRAMMAR "He descended below all things, IN THAT he comprehended all things." "In that" means by that means, or in the very act of. Descent is not the price paid for comprehension; it is the mechanism of it. He did not understand suffering from above it. He went underneath it. CROSS-REFERENCES • Alma 7:11–12 — he takes upon him our pains and sicknesses "that he may know according to the flesh how to succor his people." The same doctrine, stated experientially. • D&C 122:8 — "The Son of Man hath descended below them all. Art thou greater than he?" • Ephesians 4:9–10 — "he also descended first into the lower parts of the earth." THE COSMOLOGY (vv. 7–11) The light of Christ is the light of the sun — and "the power thereof by which it was made." The same for moon, stars, and earth. Then, with no change of gear, verse 11: "the same light that quickeneth your understandings." KEY TERM • Quicken — to make alive, to enliven. Not "to speed up." The word appears 11 times in this section and carries its dynamism: the earth is quickened (v. 26), bodies are quickened by a portion of glory (vv. 28–31), the saints are quickened and caught up (v. 96). THE CLAIM The light that makes stars burn is the light that makes you able to think. In this section that is not poetry — it is an assertion about what the universe is made of.
The hinge of the section

Verse 13

“The light which is in all things, which giveth life to all things, which is the law by which all things are governed, even the power of God who sitteth upon his throne…”
Physics= Biology= Law= Mind= A Person
If the class takes one verse home tonight, take this one. THE FIVE IDENTIFICATIONS In a single sentence, light is: • what is in all things and sustains them — physics • "which giveth life to all things" — biology • "the law by which all things are governed" — jurisprudence • (carried from v. 11) what "quickeneth your understandings" — cognition • "even the power of God who sitteth upon his throne" — a Person WHY IT MATTERS Most of the Western tradition partitions these. Natural law over here, moral law over there; science in one building, religion in another. Verse 13 refuses the partition and says they are one substance — and that substance is personal. KEY TERMS • "In the bosom of eternity" — an idiom of intimate containment; compare John 1:18, the Son "in the bosom of the Father." • Light of Christ — distinguished from the Holy Ghost; see Moroni 7:16–19 and D&C 84:45–46, "the Spirit giveth light to every man that cometh into the world." CONSEQUENCE This verse is why the section can move from planetary orbits to your bedtime without flinching. It is all one law, because it is all one light, because it is all one Person.
Verses 14–16

The Spirit and the Body Are the Soul

“And the spirit and the body are the soul of man. And the resurrection from the dead is the redemption of the soul.”
— D&C 88:15–16

What it rejects

The body as the soul’s prison. A disembodied spirit is not a freed soul — it is an incomplete one.

What it requires

Salvation is embodied. Resurrection is not an appendix to redemption; it is redemption.

Eight words that cut against roughly two thousand years of philosophy. THE STATEMENT "The spirit and the body are the soul of man" (v. 15). Not the spirit alone. The soul is the union of the two. WHAT IT REJECTS In the Platonic tradition, and in much creedal Christianity downstream of it, the body is what the soul is trying to escape, and death is the great liberation. Not here. Here the body is half the soul — which means death does not free you, it dismembers you. Compare D&C 138:50, where "the long absence of their spirits from their bodies" is regarded "as a bondage." WHAT IT REQUIRES "The resurrection from the dead is the redemption of the soul" (v. 16). Resurrection is not a bonus appended to salvation. It is salvation, completed. KEY TERMS • Soul — in this section, spirit plus body. Scripture elsewhere uses "soul" loosely to mean "spirit" (for example Alma 40:11); verse 15 is the precise definition. • Redemption — to buy back; to restore to a former standing. CROSS-REFS Moses 3:7, "man became a living soul." 2 Nephi 9:13, the restoration of spirit and body. SO WHAT This is why bodies matter to us doctrinally — why the Word of Wisdom is a commandment and not a health tip, and why verse 124 can tell you when to sleep without changing the subject.
Verses 17–20, 25–26

The Earth Keeps Its Covenant

“The earth abideth the law of a celestial kingdom, for it filleth the measure of its creation, and transgresseth not the law.”
— D&C 88:25
Dies→ Quickened→ Sanctified→ Inherited by the meek
The earth is held up as the one creation already doing what the Saints are being asked to do.
A passage we tend to skip, and it repays attention. THE CLAIM The earth is described as a covenant-keeping being. It "abideth the law of a celestial kingdom" (v. 25). It "transgresseth not the law." Therefore it will die, be quickened, be sanctified, and be inherited. THE SEQUENCE Dies (v. 26) → quickened (v. 26) → sanctified (vv. 18–20) → inherited by "the poor and the meek" (v. 17). KEY PHRASE "Filleth the measure of its creation." Measure = the full extent or design of a thing. Not exceeding what it was made for; not falling short of it. WHY THAT PHRASE IS PASTORAL It is a mercifully specific definition of a well-lived life. You are not asked to fill someone else's measure. You are asked to fill your own — which quietly addresses both over-ambition and comparison in four words. CROSS-REFS • Romans 8:22 — "the whole creation groaneth." • Moses 7:48 — the earth speaks: "when shall I rest, and be cleansed from the filthiness which is gone forth out of me?" • Matthew 5:5 — "the meek… shall inherit the earth." Verse 17 tells you when and how. RHETORICAL FUNCTION In this passage the earth is the one creation already doing what the Saints are being asked to do. It is being held up in front of them — and us.
Verses 21–24, 40

Judgment as Capacity, Not Verdict

“He who is not able to abide the law of a celestial kingdom cannot abide a celestial glory.”
— D&C 88:22
The verb is abide — to remain, to endure, to stay. The grammar is diagnostic, not punitive. Nobody is expelled from celestial glory; some are unable to remain in it.
“Intelligence cleaveth unto intelligence; wisdom receiveth wisdom; truth embraceth truth; virtue loveth virtue; light cleaveth unto light…”
— D&C 88:40
Verses 22 through 24 repeat one verb three times: abide. KEY TERM • Abide — to remain, to endure, to withstand, to stay in a place. The modern ear hears "obey"; the older and primary sense here is "to be able to stay." THE GRAMMAR IS DIAGNOSTIC "He who is not ABLE TO ABIDE the law of a celestial kingdom cannot abide a celestial glory." That is the language of capacity, not of sentencing — closer to a physician's diagnosis than a judge's ruling. Nobody in this passage is thrown out of the celestial kingdom. Some simply could not remain there. THE MECHANISM (v. 40) The law of affinity: "intelligence cleaveth unto intelligence; wisdom receiveth wisdom; truth embraceth truth; virtue loveth virtue; light cleaveth unto light." Like gathers to like, by nature. • Cleave — here, to cling or adhere to. (English "cleave" also means to split; this is the opposite sense, and the ambiguity is worth naming if someone asks.) CROSS-REF Mormon 9:4 — to dwell with God under a consciousness of your own filthiness "would be more exquisite" torment than to dwell with the damned. The same principle: glory is only glory to those who can abide it. THE URGENT QUESTION If the sorting is largely self-sorting, the question is not "will I pass?" but "what am I currently cleaving to?"
Verses 32–33

The Gift Refused

“They shall return again to their own place, to enjoy that which they are willing to receive, because they were not willing to enjoy that which they might have received.”
— D&C 88:32
“For what doth it profit a man if a gift is bestowed upon him, and he receive not the gift? Behold, he rejoices not in that which is given unto him, neither rejoices in him who is the giver of the gift.”
— D&C 88:33
Two verses, and to my mind the most merciful and the most sobering in the section. VERSE 32 — THE LIMITING FACTOR They return "to enjoy that which they are WILLING to receive, because they were NOT WILLING to enjoy that which they might have received." Read it twice. The limit is not the offer. The limit is willingness. VERSE 33 — THE SIGH "What doth it profit a man if a gift is bestowed upon him, and he receive not the gift?" Note the two verbs and who owns each: bestowed — God's act, already complete; receive — ours, still pending. THE PART THAT LANDS "Neither rejoices in HIM WHO IS THE GIVER of the gift." The real loss is not the unopened box. It is the unknown Giver. You can be the recipient of everything God has and still not know Him, because you never opened anything He sent. CONTEXT — DO NOT OVERSTATE IT In its immediate setting this describes those who "remain" (v. 32) and receive no kingdom of glory. But the principle generalizes down the whole scale, which is why it preaches so well. DISCUSSION What has already been given to you that you have not opened yet? Ask them to name one.
Verses 34–38

Law Is the Soil, Not the Fence

“That which is governed by law is also preserved by law and perfected and sanctified by the same.”
— D&C 88:34

No lawless space

“There is no space in the which there is no kingdom; and there is no kingdom in which there is no space.” (v. 37)

A law unto itself

What “seeketh to become a law unto itself… cannot be sanctified by law, neither by mercy, justice, nor judgment.” (v. 35)

We usually think of law as the fence around the field. This section says law is the soil the field is made of. VERSE 34 — THE THESIS "That which is governed by law is also PRESERVED by law and PERFECTED and SANCTIFIED by the same." Three verbs: law keeps a thing in existence, brings it to completion, and makes it holy. Law is the mechanism of growth, not the limit on it. VERSES 36–38 — NO LAWLESS SPACE "There is no space in the which there is no kingdom; and there is no kingdom in which there is no space." Space and kingdom are co-extensive. There is no unlegislated territory anywhere — not one cubic inch. • Kingdom — used far more broadly here than a political realm. Any ordered domain with a law, from a solar system to a person. VERSE 35 — THE COUNTER-CASE What "seeketh to become A LAW UNTO ITSELF… cannot be sanctified by law, neither by MERCY, justice, nor judgment." Mercy is on that list. Not because God withholds it, but because a being that has stepped outside law has stepped outside the only mechanism by which sanctification operates. The door is not locked; they have walked out of the building. CROSS-REFS • 2 Nephi 2:13 — if there is no law, there is no sin, no righteousness, no happiness, and "no God." • D&C 130:20–21 — every blessing is predicated on a law.
Verse 41 · “all things” eight times in one sentence

The Encircling God

“He comprehendeth all things, and all things are before him, and all things are round about him; and he is above all things, and in all things, and is through all things, and is round about all things; and all things are by him, and of him, even God, forever and ever.”
The syntax performs the doctrine — the sentence encircles you the way God encircles all things.
Read this one out loud, slowly. It is the rhetorical high-water mark of the section. THE COUNT Eight occurrences of "all things" in a single sentence, with seven distinct prepositions: before him, round about him, above, in, through, round about (again), by him, of him. THE DEVICE The syntax performs the doctrine. By the time you finish reading the verse aloud, you have been circled seven or eight times. It does not merely describe encompassment — it enacts it. This is worth pointing out explicitly to a class; most people feel it without noticing why. A THEOLOGICAL CARE Language like "in all things, and through all things" can sound pantheistic, as though God were identical with the universe. Verse 41 forecloses that with its last words: "even God, forever and ever." Not a force. Not a field. A Him. Compare Acts 17:28, "in him we live, and move, and have our being." CONNECTION This is verse 13's claim restated at full volume, and it sets up verse 47: to see any kingdom at all — "any or the least of these" — is to have "seen God moving in his majesty and power."
The section’s most loaded verb

Two Meanings of “Comprehend”

To encompass

v. 6 — He “descended below all things, in that he comprehended all things.”
v. 43 — the courses of the heavens “comprehend the earth and all the planets.”

To understand

v. 49 — “The light shineth in darkness, and the darkness comprehendeth it not.”
v. 49 — “The day shall come when you shall comprehend even God.”

Then verse 67 collapses both: “that body which is filled with light comprehendeth all things.”
Christ comprehends by descending. We comprehend by being filled.
The word appears seven times across six verses, and the section plays its two meanings against each other deliberately. THE TWO SENSES • To encompass, contain, surround — the older, physical sense. Verse 6 ("he comprehended all things"), verse 41, verse 43 (orbits "comprehend the earth and all the planets"). • To understand — the modern sense. Verse 48 ("was not comprehended"), verse 49 ("the darkness comprehendeth it not"). THE ALLUSION Verse 49 is quoting John 1:5. The King James "comprehended it not" is itself the same pun — the underlying Greek katelaben carries both "grasp mentally" and "seize" or "overtake." THE FUSION (v. 67) "That body which is filled with light comprehendeth all things." Here the body both contains and understands. The two senses collapse into one. THE DOCTRINE THAT FALLS OUT Christ comprehends by descending (v. 6). We comprehend by being filled (v. 67). Neither is primarily an act of intellect. Comprehension is a capacity produced by saturation — and the saturation is the part we have some say in. TEACHING NOTE This is a good place to relieve anyone who quietly believes the gospel rewards the clever. It does not. It rewards the filled.
Movement III · Verses 51–61

The Parable of the Twelve Kingdoms

A lord sends twelve servants into his field. He visits the first in the first hour, the second in the second — on to the twelfth — and tarries with each the full hour. Found nowhere else in scripture.
“And thus they all received the light of the countenance of their lord, every man in his hour, and in his time, and in his season.”
— D&C 88:58

Cosmological

Worlds without number, each in its season (v. 61).

Historical

The sequence of dispensations.

Pastoral

God’s attention is not divided — it is sequential and total.

This parable is unique to the Restoration. There is nothing like it in the Bible. THE STORY Twelve servants, one field. The lord comes to the first in the first hour and — the detail that carries the parable — "tarried with him all that hour." Then the second, the third, on to the twelfth. KEY TERMS • Tarry — to remain, to stay with. Not a passing visit; the full hour. • "The light of the countenance of their lord" — the shining of his face, an idiom of favor and presence. Numbers 6:25, "The Lord make his face shine upon thee." THREE READINGS, ALL SUPPORTED BY THE TEXT 1. Cosmological — verse 61 says it outright: "unto this parable I will liken all these kingdoms." Worlds without number (Moses 1:33), each in its season. 2. Historical — the sequence of dispensations, "every kingdom in its hour, and in its time, and in its season." 3. Pastoral — God's attention is not divided among his children. It is sequential and total. When the lord withdraws from the first servant, that is not abandonment; it is the same undivided attention, moving. VERSE 59 — AGAINST DESPAIR ABOUT YOUR PLACE IN LINE "Beginning at the first, and so on unto the last, and from the last unto the first, and from the first unto the last." The circuit runs both directions. No last is finally last. VERSE 60 — MUTUAL GLORY "That his lord might be glorified in him, and he in his lord, that they all might be glorified." Glory here is reciprocal, not handed downward. THE LINE TO LEAVE THEM WITH If it is not your hour yet, it is coming.
Movement IV · Verses 62–65

Draw Near

“Draw near unto me and I will draw near unto you; seek me diligently and ye shall find me; ask, and ye shall receive; knock, and it shall be opened unto you.”
— D&C 88:63
And the counterweight most treatments omit: “If ye ask anything that is not expedient for you, it shall turn unto your condemnation.”
— D&C 88:65
Verse 63 is the famous one, and it earns its fame. THE STRUCTURE Four conditionals stacked, every one placing the first move on us: draw near, seek diligently, ask, knock. Compare Matthew 7:7 and James 4:8, "Draw nigh to God, and he will draw nigh to you." THE FRAMING (v. 62) "Call upon me WHILE I AM NEAR." He is already close. The distance is on our side of the equation. This is not God being coy or playing hard to get. KEY TERM • Expedient — suitable, advantageous, conducive to the end in view. Not "convenient." In scripture it carries the sense of what actually serves God's purposes for you; compare John 16:7, "It is expedient for you that I go away." VERSE 65 — THE COUNTERWEIGHT "If ye ask anything that is NOT EXPEDIENT for you, it shall turn unto your CONDEMNATION." This verse is almost never quoted alongside verse 63, and it should be. Unanswered prayer is not the only risk in prayer. Answered prayer can be. WHY THIS IS PASTORAL RATHER THAN FRIGHTENING It means "thy will be done" is not a pious formality tacked onto the end of a prayer. It is the safety mechanism. Sometimes the most merciful thing God does is decline.
Verses 67–69 · The great and last promise

If Your Eye Be Single

“And if your eye be single to my glory, your whole bodies shall be filled with light, and there shall be no darkness in you; and that body which is filled with light comprehendeth all things.”
— D&C 88:67
“Sanctify yourselves that your minds become single to God, and the days will come that you shall see him; for he will unveil his face unto you… in his own time, and in his own way, and according to his own will.”
— D&C 88:68
Now the section closes a loop it opened fifty-five verses earlier. THE INCLUSIO Verse 12: light fills "the immensity of space." Verse 67: light fills a body. The cosmic aperture narrows to a personal one — same light, same physics, now inside you. KEY TERMS • Single — undivided, whole, unmixed. Not "one glance" but "one focus." The allusion is Matthew 6:22, "if therefore thine eye be single, thy whole body shall be full of light." • Sanctify (v. 68) — to make holy; to set apart for a purpose. THE MECHANISM Singleness is not the reward for being full of light. It is the aperture that lets the light in. Divided attention is a partly-closed shutter. That reframes a great deal of counsel about distraction as optical rather than merely moral — a useful thing to say to someone who feels guilty rather than obstructed. THE GREAT AND LAST PROMISE (v. 68) "The days will come that you shall see him; for he will unveil his face unto you." Then three qualifiers that matter pastorally: in his own time, in his own way, and according to his own will. Not on demand, not on your calendar — but promised. FULFILLMENT For the Kirtland Saints this was not abstract. Three years and three months later, on April 3, 1836, in the Kirtland Temple: D&C 110. Section 88 is the promise; 109 (the dedicatory prayer, itself given by revelation) and 110 are the keeping of it.
Verses 76–80, 118

Teach Ye Diligently

“Teach ye diligently and my grace shall attend you… of things both in heaven and in the earth, and under the earth; things which have been, things which are, things which must shortly come to pass… the wars and the perplexities of the nations… and a knowledge also of countries and of kingdoms.”
— D&C 88:78–79
Astronomy·Geology·History·Current events·Geopolitics·Geography
“And as all have not faith, seek ye diligently and teach one another words of wisdom; yea, seek ye out of the best books words of wisdom; seek learning, even by study and also by faith.”
— D&C 88:118
Two things here that we tend to underread. FIRST — GRACE ATTENDS TEACHING (v. 78) "Teach ye diligently and my grace shall attend you." Grace is promised to attend an act of teaching. If anyone in the room holds a calling that involves preparing lessons, that promise has their name on it. • Attend — to accompany, to go along with. SECOND — THE SYLLABUS (v. 79) Things in heaven, in the earth, and under the earth; things past, present, and "which must shortly come to pass"; things at home and abroad; "the wars and the perplexities of the nations"; "a knowledge also of countries and of kingdoms." That is astronomy, geology, history, current events, geopolitics, and geography — assigned by revelation in 1832, to a frontier congregation. THE MOTIVE (v. 80) Not enrichment. Readiness: "that ye may be PREPARED IN ALL THINGS when I shall send you again." VERSE 118 — THE METHOD, AND ITS FRAMING "AND AS ALL HAVE NOT FAITH, seek ye diligently and teach one another words of wisdom; yea, seek ye out of the best books words of wisdom; seek learning, even by study and also by faith." Do not skip the first clause. Study is introduced as the accommodation for a community whose faith is uneven. It is not named the lesser path — but it is named the accessible one. The door that stays open on the days faith is thin. HISTORICAL NOTE This is the charter of the School of the Prophets, which convened in Kirtland in January 1833 and studied grammar, geography, and Hebrew alongside theology. The breadth of verse 79 was taken literally, and quickly.

THE SIGNS (vv. 87–91) The earth reels "to and fro as a drunken man"; the sun hides his face; the moon is "bathed in blood"; the stars fall "as a fig that falleth from off a fig tree"; men's hearts fail them. The vocabulary is Isaiah and Revelation throughout — Isaiah 24:20, Isaiah 34:4, Revelation 6:12–13. THE ORDER OF EVENTS (v. 88) — NOT THE ONE WE EXPECT "AFTER YOUR TESTIMONY cometh wrath and indignation upon the people." Testimony precedes judgment. Which is why verse 81 draws the practical consequence: "it becometh every man who hath been warned to warn his neighbour." The warning is itself the mercy. THE SEVEN TRUMPS AND THE ORDER OF RESURRECTION First (vv. 96–98) — celestial; "they are Christ's, the first fruits." Second (v. 99) — those who received the gospel in spirit prison. Third (vv. 100–101) — those under condemnation; "they live not again until the thousand years are ended." Fourth (v. 102) — those who "remain filthy still." Fifth (vv. 103–104) — the everlasting gospel to all nations; "every knee shall bow." Sixth (v. 105) — "She is fallen, is fallen!" (Babylon; compare Revelation 18:2) Seventh (v. 106) — "It is finished! The Lamb of God hath overcome and trodden the wine-press alone." KEY TERMS • Trump — trumpet; in scripture the instrument of assembly, alarm, and enthronement. • Wine-press — Isaiah 63:3, treading grapes as an image of judgment: "I have trodden the winepress alone." • Michael, "the seventh angel, even the archangel" (v. 112) — identified in Restoration scripture as Adam (D&C 107:54; 128:21). THE DETAIL WORTH THE WHOLE SLIDE (vv. 108–110) The trumps sound AGAIN, and this time each reveals "the secret acts of men, and the thoughts and intents of their hearts, and the mighty works of God" for its thousand years, in order. That is a remarkable model of final judgment. Not primarily a sentencing — a DISCLOSURE. History read back aloud, in full, with nothing left out, including "the mighty works of God," most of which no one alive has ever seen.

Verse 119. Eight uses of the word "house" in a single verse. THE SEVEN DESCRIPTORS Prayer, fasting, faith, LEARNING, glory, order, God. WHERE LEARNING FALLS Fourth of seven. Dead center, flanked by faith on one side and glory on the other. The house of God is a school — and the section makes that literal in verses 127–141, which legislate the School of the Prophets: who stands where, how they greet one another, the ordinance of washing feet. THE COMMANDS THAT PRECEDE IT "Organize yourselves; prepare every needful thing." Compare verse 74: "assemble yourselves together, and organize yourselves, and prepare yourselves, and sanctify yourselves; yea, purify your hearts." The verbs escalate from logistics to holiness — and they are in that order deliberately. THE TIMING Given January 1833. The Kirtland Temple was dedicated March 27, 1836 — three years and three months later. Section 88 is the commission; sections 109 and 110 are the fulfillment, including the appearance of the Savior, which is verse 68's great and last promise, kept. WHERE THEY HAVE SEEN THIS VERSE It is inscribed, in whole or in part, in Latter-day Saint temples. Members often recognize the cadence without knowing it comes from section 88 — worth pointing out, because it connects the reading to a building they have stood in.

This is the tonal turn, and it is the most important structural feature of the section. THE TITLE, EXPLAINED Both halves are lifted from the text. "The immensity of space" is verse 12, verbatim. "Bedtime" is verse 124: "retire to thy bed early… arise early." The title names the section's two endpoints — the largest scale imaginable and the most domestic detail of a person's day. THE DISTANCE — STATED PRECISELY The apocalyptic material runs through verse 116 ("they shall not any more see death"). Then: call your solemn assembly (117), seek learning out of the best books (118), establish a house (119), and by verse 124 you are being told when to go to bed. Eight verses from the last apocalyptic line to bedtime. Not instantaneous — but startlingly tight. WHY IT IS NOT AN ANTICLIMAX It is the argument. The same law that fixes the courses of the planets (v. 43) governs when you sleep (v. 124) — and the stated reason is practical: "that your bodies and your minds may be INVIGORATED." Fit for the work. Recall verse 15: if the body is half the soul, sleep is a spiritual matter, not merely a biological one. VERSE 122 — A CHARTER FOR HOW A CLASS RUNS "Let NOT ALL BE SPOKESMEN AT ONCE; but let ONE SPEAK AT A TIME and let ALL LISTEN unto his sayings, that when all have spoken THAT ALL MAY BE EDIFIED OF ALL, and that every man may have an EQUAL PRIVILEGE." Read that in your next teacher council. It anticipates almost every modern rule of good discussion facilitation. • Edify — to build up. Same root as "edifice." The house imagery of verse 119 is still running underneath. VERSE 125 — ABOVE ALL THINGS "Clothe yourselves with the bond of charity, as with a MANTLE, which is the bond of perfectness and peace." • Mantle — an outer cloak. It goes on over everything else, and it is the thing others see first. Compare Colossians 3:14, charity as "the bond of perfectness." "Above all things" is not "among other things." It is a ranking.
Verses 127–141 · The School of the Prophets

Friendship as an Ordinance

“I salute you in the name of the Lord Jesus Christ… in a determination that is fixed, immovable, and unchangeable, to be your friend and brother through the grace of God in the bonds of love, to walk in all the commandments of God blameless, in thanksgiving, forever and ever.”
— D&C 88:133
The section closes with the washing of feet — “according to the pattern given in the thirteenth chapter of John’s testimony” (v. 141).
The last movement legislates the School of the Prophets, and one detail deserves attention. THE SALUTATION (v. 133) The prescribed greeting between members of the school is, on inspection, a covenant of friendship: "in a determination that is FIXED, IMMOVABLE, AND UNCHANGEABLE, to be your friend and brother through the grace of God in the bonds of love, to walk in all the commandments of God blameless, in thanksgiving, forever and ever." Friendship is made a formal ordinance — not a pleasantry, but a covenant, in the same register scripture reserves for the everlasting covenant itself. KEY TERMS • Salute — to greet formally. The salutation here is liturgical, not casual. • "In token or remembrance of the everlasting covenant" — token, a sign or outward evidence. The phrase recurs through verses 131–133. • Solemn assembly (vv. 70, 117) — a formal convocation for sacred instruction and ordinances. The term carries into modern practice, in the sustaining of a new President of the Church. THE WASHING OF FEET (vv. 139–141) Instituted here as the ordinance of admission to the school, "according to the pattern given in the thirteenth chapter of John's testimony" — the Last Supper, where Jesus washed his disciples' feet (John 13:4–17). Verse 141 specifies it begins with prayer and follows the partaking of bread and wine. THE POINT The School of the Prophets is not framed as a curriculum. It is framed as discipleship, in the posture of the room where the Savior knelt down in front of his friends. The longest revelation in the Doctrine and Covenants ends with a basin and a towel.
The governing thesis

This Is the Olive Leaf

Section 88 answers Section 87’s prophecy of war not by denying the war, but by revealing what holds while it happens.
Light fills all space. Law governs every kingdom. And in the middle of the earthquakes and the trumps, the Saints are told to organize themselves, establish a house of learning and order and glory, teach one another, speak one at a time, go to bed early, and put on charity like a coat.
Not the announcement that the flood is over — the evidence that something is alive inside it, and the instructions for building on the ground as it dries.
Let's land it. THE ANSWER TO SECTION 87 Section 88 answers the prophecy of war — but notice HOW. It does not say the war will not come. It says the war is not the deepest thing that is true. WHAT HOLDS • Light fills all space (v. 12). • Law governs every kingdom, and there is no lawless space (vv. 36–38). • Christ is in the sun and in your understanding (vv. 7, 11). • The earth keeps its covenant and fills the measure of its creation (v. 25). • Every servant gets his full hour (v. 58). • The gift has already been bestowed; the only question is whether it gets opened (v. 33). WHAT WE ARE TOLD TO DO And then, in the middle of the earthquakes and the trumps, the instructions are startlingly ordinary. Organize yourselves. Build a house of learning. Teach each other. Don't all talk at once. Go to bed. Get up. Put on charity like a coat. THE IMAGE, CLOSED Noah's dove did not bring news that the flood was over — the ark was still floating. It brought a leaf: evidence that somewhere, something was alive and growing, and that there would be ground to build on. Section 88 is that leaf. And the last thing it tells us to do is build. Bear testimony and close.
