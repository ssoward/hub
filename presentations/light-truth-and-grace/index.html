BYU Devotional · July 7, 2009

Light, Truth,
and Grace

Three Interrelated Principles Necessary for Exaltation
Richard D. Draper
Professor of Ancient Scripture, Brigham Young University
Welcome. Tonight we work through Richard D. Draper's 2009 BYU devotional, "Light, Truth, and Grace: Three Interrelated Principles Necessary for Exaltation." WHY THIS TALK Draper takes three words that ordinary gospel conversation treats as near-synonyms for "goodness" — light, truth, grace — and shows they are three distinct things standing in a fixed causal order. By the end you should be able to say what each one is and how they connect. THE THESIS IN ONE SENTENCE Grace flows universally and unconditionally; it carries light, which is not information but capacity; capacity is what makes truth receivable; and truth — not light — is what glorifies. SPEAKER Richard D. Draper was a professor of Ancient Scripture at BYU and served in Religious Education there. This is a summer devotional address, so the register is scholarly but personal. A NOTE ON DIFFICULTY Be honest with your class up front: this is an abstract talk, and Draper himself says so. He opens by admitting he could not understand the scriptures at its center. That admission is the doorway — use it. KEY TERMS • Exaltation — in Latter-day Saint usage, the highest degree of the celestial kingdom: eternal life in God's presence, with the capacity to continue progressing. Broader than "salvation," which can mean resurrection and redemption generally. Draper's subtitle claims these three principles are necessary for it. • Devotional — a BYU campus address. Doctrinally serious, but a professor's exposition, not official Church instruction. Say so if anyone asks whether this is binding. • Ancient Scripture — the BYU department covering Old Testament, New Testament, and the ancient context of Restoration scripture. Draper's field is why his instinct is to reach for a period dictionary. Press 'S' for speaker notes. Arrow keys to navigate.
How the talk opens

Two Baffling Scriptures

D&C 88:6–7

“He descended below all things… that he might be in all and through all things, the light of truth; which truth shineth.”

Just how does truth shine?

D&C 93:42

The Lord rebukes Frederick G. Williams for “not [having] taught [his] children light and truth.”

I can teach truth — but how do I teach light?

“I have read [some] over and over, and I still don’t understand what they are saying.”
Draper opens with an admission that a lot of teachers would not make: there are scriptures he has read over and over and still does not understand. THE TWO PUZZLES • D&C 88:6–7 — Christ descended below all things so He might be "in all and through all things, the light of truth; which truth shineth." Draper's question is grammatical and sincere: light shines, obviously. But how does TRUTH shine? What kind of thing is truth, that it can radiate? • D&C 93:42 — the Lord's rebuke to Frederick G. Williams, one of his counselors in the First Presidency: "You have not taught your children light and truth." Draper's question: I know how to teach my children truth. What would it even mean to teach them light? WHY START HERE Both puzzles have the same shape. Each treats light as though it were a THING that can be possessed, transmitted, and taught — not merely a metaphor for understanding. The rest of the talk is an answer to "what kind of thing?" HISTORICAL NOTE Frederick G. Williams was Second Counselor in the First Presidency when section 93 was given (May 1833). Verses 41–50 rebuke, in turn, Joseph Smith, Sidney Rigdon, Newel K. Whitney, and Williams — a reminder that the section's soaring Christology lands in very domestic correction about how men were raising their families. TEACHING TIP Ask the class Draper's second question before you go on: "How would you teach a child light?" Let the silence sit. The talk is the answer. KEY TERMS • Frederick G. Williams (1787–1842) — a physician who joined the Church in 1830 and served as Second Counselor to Joseph Smith in the First Presidency from 1833 to 1837. The rebuke in 93:42 is directed at a member of the highest council in the Church, about how he is raising his family. • Castigate — Draper's word for what the Lord does in 93:42: to rebuke severely. • "The light of truth" — the exact phrase in D&C 93:29, which becomes central on slide 13. Note it now; it is not the same as "light and truth." THE CONTEXT PEOPLE MISS Section 93 was given May 6, 1833, in Kirtland. Its first 20 verses are among the loftiest Christology in scripture — the Testimony of John, the Word, grace for grace. Then verses 41–50 turn and rebuke, by name, Joseph Smith, Sidney Rigdon, Newel K. Whitney, and Frederick G. Williams — mostly about their families and their households. The section moves from the premortal Christ to "set in order your own house" (v. 43) in the space of a few verses. That descent from cosmology to the domestic is the same move D&C 88 makes, and it is worth naming if your class studied that section.
Where understanding came

A Laundromat in Tempe

Waiting for his clothes to dry between sessions of a symposium, he read Doctrine and Covenants 93.

“A number of scriptures slotted themselves into place and I understood.”
Years after he first puzzled over the verses as a junior at BYU.
This detail is not filler, and I would not skip it. THE SETTING Draper was in Tempe, Arizona, presenting at a week-long Church Educational System symposium. He went to a nearby Laundromat to do washing, and while waiting for the dryer he read section 93. That is where it landed — years after first hitting the problem as a junior at BYU. WHY IT MATTERS STRUCTURALLY The setting performs the doctrine he is about to teach. If light is capacity granted by grace, rather than a reward earned by scholarly effort, then it should be expected to arrive unearned, in an undignified place, to a man who had already given up on the question. A Laundromat is exactly the right place for this talk's thesis to be proven. THE COMPANION MOVE Just before this he makes the "wavicle" joke — he took astronomy as a sophomore, learned light is neither particle nor wave but a "wavicle," and says "I have no idea what that is." Self-deprecation right before an abstract lecture. He knows he is about to be difficult, and he is buying goodwill. PASTORAL USE For anyone in the class who feels that spiritual understanding belongs to scholars and not to them: a religion professor could not crack this for years, and then it came to him in a laundromat. That is the talk's implicit promise. KEY TERMS • Church Educational System (CES) — the Church's network of seminaries, institutes of religion, and schools. Draper was in Tempe presenting at a week-long CES symposium, which is why he was out of town with laundry to do. • Wavicle — an informal, semi-joking term for light's dual particle-and-wave behavior. It is not standard physics vocabulary, which is exactly Draper's point: he was handed a word that explained nothing. "I have no idea what that is." • Wave–particle duality — the actual phenomenon: light behaves as a wave in some experiments and as discrete particles (photons) in others. Worth a sentence if someone asks, but do not let the class detour into physics — Draper's interest is that the physical definition left him no closer to the scripture. A CONNECTION WORTH DRAWING There is a small irony he does not name. Physics could not tell him what light is in the sense he needed, because he was asking a different question than physics asks. The scriptures define light functionally — by what it DOES to a person — not by what it is made of. That distinction is the whole method of the talk.
The verse everything hangs on

D&C 93:36

“The glory of God is intelligence, or, in other words, light and truth.”
Familiar enough to be a campus slogan — and, Draper admits, baffling.
To read it as a definition rather than a saying, you must first know what glory means.
Here is the verse the entire talk is organized around. Most people at BYU can recite it. Draper's point is that reciting it and understanding it are different things. WHY IT IS BAFFLING Read it as a chain of equations: glory = intelligence = light and truth. Every term in that chain is doing unfamiliar work. "Intelligence" does not obviously mean IQ. "Light" does not obviously mean photons. And "glory" — if glory means praise or approval, which is how most of Christian history has read it — then the verse says God's approval is intelligence, which is nearly nonsense. THE METHOD Draper's move is to treat the verse as a DEFINITION rather than an aphorism. Definitions can be unpacked term by term. So the next several slides do exactly that, starting with the term that unlocks the rest: glory. SET THE EXPECTATION Tell the class we are going to spend three slides on the word "glory" before we get to light. That patience is what makes the payoff work. KEY TERMS • Intelligence — carries two distinct senses in section 93, which is a large part of why the verse is hard. Preview only; slide 13 unpacks it. Here it is the abstract sense: "light and truth." • "Or, in other words" — the verse's own signal that it is offering a definition, not a poetic restatement. Draper leans on this; it is the textual warrant for treating the whole line as an equation. • Glory — the term we spend the next three slides on. Everything depends on which sense is meant. WHERE THE VERSE SITS D&C 93:36 is almost always quoted alone, which strips it. In context it is the head of a tight run: v. 36 defines glory; v. 37 says "light and truth forsake that evil one"; v. 38 describes innocence; v. 39 explains that the wicked one takes away light and truth "through disobedience"; v. 40 commands parents to raise children "in light and truth." So the campus-slogan verse is actually the opening premise of an argument about how light and truth are gained and lost — and it lands on child-rearing. TEACHING TIP Read verses 36 through 40 aloud as a block. Most classes have never heard 36 in its setting, and hearing it run into 40 changes what it sounds like.
The lexical argument

Glory: Approval, or Radiance?

Milton, Johnson, Aquinas, and C. S. Lewis all read scriptural glory as approbation — honor, renown, God’s favor. Draper turns instead to the dictionary available to Joseph Smith.
Webster’s 1828 — “glory” (noun)
1.“Brightness; luster; splendor.”
2.“Splendor; magnificence.”
3.The halo of rays around a figure’s head in art
4.Praise and honor in worship
5.Renown, fame, celebrity
Radiance is not merely primary — it occupies the first three entries. Fame is fifth.
This slide is the hinge of the first half. If it does not land, nothing after it works. THE TRADITION Draper names Milton, Samuel Johnson, Thomas Aquinas, and C. S. Lewis as representatives of the dominant Christian reading: scriptural "glory" means approbation — praise, honor, renown, the favor God grants or receives. On that reading, "the glory of God" is about esteem. THE COUNTER-ARGUMENT He turns to Webster's 1828 dictionary — the one in Joseph Smith's linguistic world — where "glory" is FIRST "brightness; luster; splendor," and only later fame or praise. He notes the 1828 edition also says that in a scriptural sense, glory is a manifestation of the presence of God. VERIFIED, AND STRONGER THAN HE CLAIMS I checked the 1828 entry. Draper says fame and praise are "secondary." They are actually fourth and fifth. Radiance occupies the first three entries outright. His case is stronger than he states it. WHY THE STAKES ARE HIGH If glory means approval, D&C 93:36 is unintelligible — approval is not intelligence. If glory means radiance, and light is a constituent of glory, then the verse becomes a definition you can actually unpack. This single lexical move is what makes the rest of the talk possible. METHOD NOTE FOR THE CLASS This is a good moment to show what careful scripture study looks like: when a verse resists you, one legitimate move is to ask what the words meant to the person who received them. KEY TERMS • Approbation — approval, commendation, official sanction. This is the sense of "glory" Draper is arguing against. • Webster's 1828 — Noah Webster's "American Dictionary of the English Language," the standard American reference of Joseph Smith's era and region. Using it is a recognized method for reading the Doctrine and Covenants. • Lexical — having to do with words and their meanings. A lexical argument is one that turns on what a word meant, when. • Luster / splendor — brilliance, brightness. Both are radiance words, not honor words. WHO THE FOUR ARE (in case someone asks) • John Milton (1608–1674) — poet, "Paradise Lost." • Samuel Johnson (1709–1784) — compiler of the great 1755 English dictionary; a lexicographer, which makes his reading weighty. • Thomas Aquinas (1225–1274) — the central theologian of medieval Catholicism. • C. S. Lewis (1898–1963) — whose essay "The Weight of Glory" wrestles with exactly this ambiguity: whether glory means fame and approval or luminosity. Lewis takes both seriously, so Draper's grouping is a little brisk — worth mentioning if you have a Lewis reader in the room. THE METHODOLOGICAL CAUTION A period dictionary tells you what a word could mean in 1828; it does not by itself prove which sense a revelation intends. What makes Draper's case stick is not the dictionary alone but the dictionary PLUS the scriptural pattern on the next two slides. Say that — it keeps the class from thinking a dictionary settles doctrine.
Moses 1:2–5

Glory Is What Made Moses Able

“And he saw God face to face, and he talked with him, and the glory of God was upon Moses; therefore Moses could endure his presence.”
— Moses 1:2
Glory is not the reward for the interview. It is the equipment that made the interview survivable.
“No man can behold all my works, except he behold all my glory; … and afterwards remain in the flesh on the earth.”
— Moses 1:5
This is the strongest scriptural evidence for reading glory as enabling power rather than conferred honor, and it turns on one word. THE LOAD-BEARING WORD "The glory of God was upon Moses; THEREFORE Moses could endure his presence." Therefore. The glory is the cause; the enduring is the effect. Moses was certainly under God's favor — but favor is not what the verse says made the encounter survivable. Glory did. THE SECOND WITNESS (Moses 1:4–5) God tells Moses He will show him the workmanship of His hands, "but not all, for my works are without end." Why not all? "No man can behold all my works, except he behold all my glory… and afterwards remain in the flesh." Beholding all of God's works requires beholding all His glory — and that would require such a change in a person's constitution that he could no longer remain mortal. DRAPER'S INFERENCE It is God's glory that gives Him the capacity to be all-seeing. Glory is not decoration on omniscience; it is the substrate of it. THE STRUCTURAL RHYME — POINT THIS OUT Glory capacitates Moses to endure God's presence. In four slides, light will capacitate the mind to receive truth. Same mechanism, two scales. Draper has found one pattern operating at both, and that is why the talk holds together. KEY TERMS • Capacitate — to make capable; to supply capacity. Draper uses this word repeatedly and it is the hinge of his whole definition of light. It is a real English word, but uncommon enough that a class may need it said plainly: to capacitate is to make able. • Endure (Moses 1:2) — here it means to withstand or survive, not to tolerate patiently. Moses could survive the encounter. • "Remain in the flesh" (Moses 1:5) — stay mortal. Beholding all of God's glory would change a person's constitution past the point of mortality. • Transfiguration — the temporary change that allows a mortal to endure God's presence. This is the technical term for what happens to Moses, and to Joseph Smith in the First Vision. WHERE MOSES 1 COMES FROM The vision of Moses was received by Joseph Smith in June 1830 and now stands as the first chapter of the Book of Moses in the Pearl of Great Price. It is not in the Bible. Worth noting: Draper's strongest evidence for glory-as-capacity comes from Restoration scripture, which is part of why this reading is not common outside it. CROSS-REFERENCES • Moses 1:11 — "my spiritual eyes, for my natural eyes could not have beheld; for I should have withered and died in his presence." • JS—H 1:17 — Joseph is delivered from the enemy and then sees the vision; D&C 67:11 — "neither can any natural man abide the presence of God, neither after the carnal mind." Both say the same thing: unaided mortality cannot survive the encounter. Something must be added first.
The witness of scripture

Glory Appears as Burning Light

Israel

Sinai like a devouring fire (Ex. 24:17). “The Lord thy God is a consuming fire” (Deut. 4:24). The pillar of fire; the cloud filling the tabernacle.

Joseph Smith

“A pillar of light… above the brightness of the sun.” Two Personages “whose brightness and glory defy all description” (JS—H 1:16–17).

Kirtland & the future

“His eyes were as a flame of fire; … his countenance shone above the brightness of the sun” (D&C 110:3). He comes “clothed in the brightness of his glory” (D&C 65:5).

Past, present, and future: wherever God’s presence is manifest, it is manifest as radiance.
Having made the lexical case, Draper piles up the scriptural witness. Move briskly here — the point is cumulative weight, not close reading of any one text. ANCIENT ISRAEL • Sinai: the display on the mountain was "like devouring fire" (Exodus 24:17). • Moses: "The Lord thy God is a consuming fire" (Deuteronomy 4:24). • The pillar of fire, which "gave light" to Israel but troubled the Egyptians (Exodus 13:21; 14:24). • The cloud of glory on the tabernacle, its radiance filling the court (Exodus 40:34). THE RESTORATION • First Vision: "a pillar of light… above the brightness of the sun"; and in another account, "surrounded with a brilliant light which eclipsed the sun at noon day." • Kirtland Temple, D&C 110:3: "His eyes were as a flame of fire… his countenance shone above the brightness of the sun." STILL FUTURE • D&C 84:5 — a house shall be built, and "a cloud shall rest upon it, which cloud shall be even the glory of the Lord, which shall fill the house." • D&C 65:5 — at the Second Coming He comes "clothed in the brightness of his glory." THE CONCLUSION This is not a poetic convention that happened to persist. Across three dispensations and into the future, the manifest presence of God is radiance. Which means "the glory of God is… light and truth" is describing something real, not paying a compliment. KEY TERMS • Tabernacle — the portable sanctuary Israel carried through the wilderness; the predecessor of the temple. Exodus 40:34 has the cloud covering it while the glory filled it. • Shekhinah — the later rabbinic term for God's visible dwelling-presence among His people. The word is not in the Bible, but the concept is exactly what these cloud-and-fire passages describe. Useful to name, because it shows this reading is not a Latter-day Saint invention — Judaism developed vocabulary for it too. • Consuming fire (Deut. 4:24) — an image of God's presence as something that burns; the same presence that warms and lights can also destroy what cannot abide it. Ties directly to the capacity theme. • Pillar of fire / pillar of light — the guiding manifestation of God's presence (Exodus 13:21), echoed in Joseph Smith's "pillar of light" (JS—H 1:16). The parallel is almost certainly deliberate in Joseph's telling. THE DETAIL WORTH POINTING OUT Exodus 14:24 — the same pillar "gave light" to Israel and troubled the Egyptians. One phenomenon, two opposite effects, depending on who is standing in it. That is the whole doctrine of this talk in a single verse: the same light that capacitates one person condemns another, and the difference is in the receiver. CROSS-REFERENCE D&C 84:5 — the promise that a house shall be built and "a cloud shall rest upon it, which cloud shall be even the glory of the Lord." Draper cites this to show the phenomenon is still future, not merely ancient.
D&C 88:11–13

So What Is Light?

“The light which shineth, which giveth you light, is through him who enlighteneth your eyes, which is the same light that quickeneth your understandings.”
“…the light which is in all things, which giveth life to all things, which is the law by which all things are governed.”
— D&C 88:11, 13
“Living and capacitating energy”
Not merely luminosity — an ever-present power that enlightens, organizes, capacitates, and quickens as it radiates out from God.
Now the payoff of the first half. Draper builds his definition inductively from D&C 88 — the section we studied in the companion presentation. WHAT THE VERSES ACTUALLY SAY • v. 11 — light makes vision possible AND "quickeneth your understandings." It activates the intellect. • v. 13 — light "is in all things," "giveth life to all things," and "is the law by which all things are governed." • v. 12 — it "proceedeth forth from the presence of God to fill the immensity of space." THE DEFINITION HE LANDS ON "An ever-present, life- and law-inducing power that manifests itself, among other ways, as natural light, intellectual activity, and the living energy in all things." Compressed: LIVING AND CAPACITATING ENERGY. KEY TERM • Quicken — to make alive, to enliven. Not "speed up." Light does not merely illuminate what is already there; it makes the perceiver alive to it. THE GROWTH PROMISE (D&C 50:24) "That which is of God is light; and he that receiveth light, AND CONTINUETH IN GOD, receiveth more light; and that light groweth brighter and brighter until the perfect day." Note the middle clause — we come back to it when we deal with obedience. AND THE DESTINATION (D&C 88:67) "If your eye be single to my glory, your whole bodies shall be filled with light… and that body which is filled with light comprehendeth all things." As one increases in light, one increases in ABILITY, until able to comprehend all things.
The talk’s central analogy

The Pint and the Quart

A pint of truth
→
A quart of truth
“If my spiritual mind is pint-sized, I can only hold a pint of truth… If God wants to give me a quart of truth, He sends the light that enlarges my capacity.”
Light is not the water. Light is what makes the jar bigger.
This is the single most portable thing in the talk. If your class remembers one image, make it this one. THE ANALOGY "If my spiritual mind is pint-sized, I can only hold a pint of truth. If God is to give me more, the capacity of my mind must be expanded. Light does the expansion. It enlarges, as it were, my mind. Thus if God wants to give me a quart of truth, He sends the light that enlarges my capacity." THE DISTINCTION IT PROTECTS Light is not the contents. Light is the container's capacity. Almost every casual use of "light and truth" collapses the two into synonyms for knowledge — and once you do that, the whole architecture of this talk becomes invisible. THE PASTORAL PAYOFF — DO NOT SKIP THIS If light were information, spiritual growth would be an accumulation problem: read more, study harder, know more. If light is CAPACITY, spiritual growth is a container problem — and the container is enlarged by God, not by effort. So to the person who says "I read the scriptures and I just don't get it" — the answer is not "try harder." The answer is that they have accurately described a capacity, and God has undertaken to expand it. That reframes discouragement completely. DISCUSSION Ask: can you name a time when a passage that meant nothing to you at twenty meant everything at forty? Same words. Different jar. KEY TERMS • Capacity — the amount a vessel can hold. In this analogy it is the variable; the truth on offer is not. • Analogy (not allegory) — an analogy makes ONE point of comparison. This one compares capacity. It is not a complete model of the spiritual life, and it breaks if pressed. WHERE THE ANALOGY BREAKS — SAY THIS IF SOMEONE PUSHES Taken too far, the image suggests God is rationing truth and waiting for us to qualify for more. That is not Draper's claim. His claim is the opposite: the truth is already "plainly manifest" (D&C 93:31), and the constraint is entirely on the receiving end. Nobody is being held back; some are simply not yet able to hold what is already being offered. Also resist making light sound like a substance poured into a jar. Light IS the expansion of the jar, not the contents. If the class starts imagining two liquids, redraw it. CROSS-REFERENCES • D&C 50:24 — "that light groweth brighter and brighter until the perfect day." The jar keeps growing; there is no final size. • Alma 12:9–10 — "he that will harden his heart, the same receiveth the lesser portion of the word… until they know nothing." The same mechanism running in reverse: capacity can shrink. Alma 12 is the mirror image of this slide and is worth reading aloud alongside it.
D&C 93:28, 24

Truth Is What Glorifies

“He that keepeth his commandments receiveth truth and light, until he is glorified in truth and knoweth all things.”
— D&C 93:28

Light

The means. Capacity, power, the enlarged container.

Truth

The end. “Knowledge of things as they are, and as they were, and as they are to come” (93:24) — the sum of existence.

A crucial correction that is easy to miss, because we habitually pair the two words and stop thinking. THE CLAIM "One is not, however, glorified in light — or, as here defined, in power or energy. Exaltation is contingent upon the reception of the other, all-important element of which glory is composed, namely, truth." You are not exalted by capacity. You are exalted by what fills it. D&C 93:28 says it precisely: keep the commandments, receive truth and light, "until he is GLORIFIED IN TRUTH." THE DEFINITION OF TRUTH (93:24) "Knowledge of things as they are, and as they were, and as they are to come." Past, present, future — the whole of what is. THE HYMN Draper glosses this with a Latter-day Saint hymn: "the sum of existence." That is "Oh Say, What Is Truth?" — "Truth is the sum of existence." Worth quoting or even singing a line, because it makes the abstract definition memorable. WHY THE ORDER MATTERS Light without truth is an empty jar, however large. Truth without light is water with nowhere to go. The talk needs both, in that order, and this slide is where the order is fixed. KEY TERMS • Glorified — brought into glory. Given the definition established earlier, to be "glorified in truth" is to be filled with, and made radiant by, the knowledge of what is. • Fulness — the complete measure, not a partial share. The word recurs through section 93 (vv. 12, 13, 16, 19, 20, 26) and is the destination of the whole process. • Truth (D&C 93:24) — "knowledge of things as they are, and as they were, and as they are to come." Note this is a definition of truth as KNOWLEDGE of reality, which sets up the puzzle on slide 13 where truth also seems to mean reality itself. • Contingent — dependent upon. Draper: exaltation is "contingent upon the reception of… truth." THE HYMN "Oh Say, What Is Truth?" — Hymn 272, text by John Jaques (1827–1900), an English convert who wrote it as a young man before emigrating. The line Draper alludes to: "Truth is the sum of existence." Jaques crossed the plains with the Martin handcart company and lost his daughter and mother in that crossing, which gives the hymn's confidence in truth a considerable weight. Quoting a line, or noting the author's story, makes the abstract definition land. CROSS-REFERENCE John 8:32 — "ye shall know the truth, and the truth shall make you free." Same ordering: knowing precedes the change.
D&C 84:44–47

Why Obedience Is Required

“For the word of the Lord is truth, and whatsoever is truth is light, and whatsoever is light is Spirit, even the Spirit of Jesus Christ.”
— D&C 84:45
Word=Truth=Light=Spirit
Because Christ alone controls their dissemination, He may set the terms. Draper’s ordering: first obedience to the word, then light, and finally truth.
Why should obedience have anything to do with an increase in capacity? Draper's answer runs through a chain of identities. THE CHAIN (D&C 84:45) "The word of the Lord is truth, and whatsoever is truth is light, and whatsoever is light is Spirit, even the Spirit of Jesus Christ." Word = truth = light = Spirit. These are not four analogies; they are one thing under four names. DRAPER'S GLOSS "They are inseparably welded together so that a person cannot be touched by one without being touched by all." THE ARGUMENT FOR OBEDIENCE D&C 84:44 — "you shall live by every word that proceedeth forth from the mouth of God." If you want quality of life, be obedient. And because Christ alone controls the dissemination of light and truth, through the bestowal of His Spirit, He is entitled to stipulate the terms on which they are granted. THE ORDERING "But first comes obedience to the word, then light, and finally truth." FLAG THIS NOW — WE RETURN TO IT Hold that ordering loosely. In the summary, Draper will say light is "freely manifest unto all" through grace, prior to any obedience. Those two statements are reconcilable, and slide 21 does the reconciling. If someone in your class is sharp, they will catch it here — tell them we will come back. A SUPPORT DRAPER DOESN'T CITE D&C 93:39 is his strongest proof text and he never quotes it: "that wicked one cometh and taketh away light and truth, THROUGH DISOBEDIENCE, from the children of men." That is the obedience link stated flatly, in the very section he is expounding. KEY TERMS • Hearken (D&C 84:46–47) — to hear with the intent to obey. Not merely to listen. The verse promises light to "every man that cometh into the world," but enlightenment specifically to those "that hearkeneth to the voice of the Spirit." That distinction is doing quiet work — note it, because slide 20 depends on it. • Dissemination — distribution, spreading abroad. Draper's point is that Christ controls the distribution, and therefore may set terms. • Requisite — required; necessary as a condition. • Welded — Draper's metaphor for word/truth/light/Spirit: not glued together but fused into one piece. You cannot pick up one end without lifting all of it. THE ARGUMENT IN PLAIN FORM 1. The word of the Lord is truth (84:45). 2. Truth is light. Light is Spirit. Spirit is the Spirit of Jesus Christ. 3. Therefore obeying the word is not a separate transaction that earns light — obeying the word IS how you take hold of the thing light already is. Framed that way, obedience stops sounding like a toll and starts sounding like a grip. CROSS-REFERENCES • D&C 93:39–40 — light and truth taken away "through disobedience"; and the command to bring up children in light and truth. • John 7:17 — "If any man will do his will, he shall know of the doctrine." Doing precedes knowing. The same ordering Draper claims.
D&C 131:7–8

Light Is Substance, Not Metaphor

“All spirit is matter.”  Therefore the bestowal of the Spirit is “an impartation of actual celestial substance — actual elements of spirit producing higher power, higher capacity, higher life.”
— D&C 131:7–8, with Draper’s gloss
Most Christian theology reads light-language as figurative. Joseph Smith’s material monism makes it literal: to receive the Spirit is to receive something.
This is the moment the talk becomes distinctively Restoration theology, and it is worth marking explicitly. THE SCRIPTURE D&C 131:7–8: "There is no such thing as immaterial matter. All spirit is matter, but it is more fine or pure, and can only be discerned by purer eyes." DRAPER'S INFERENCE If the Spirit of Christ is included in "all spirit," then its bestowal on a person is "an impartation of actual celestial substance — actual elements of spirit producing higher power, higher capacity, higher life." WHY THIS IS A BIG DEAL Almost all of Christian theology treats light-language about God as figurative — beautiful metaphor for understanding or holiness. Joseph Smith's material monism refuses the immaterial altogether. On this view, the capacity enlargement we have been discussing is not a figure of speech. Something is actually added to you. This is the point at which Draper's argument stops being available to most of Christendom. Say so — it helps a class understand what is distinctive about Restoration doctrine rather than assuming everyone teaches this. THE WELD (D&C 88:66) "My voice is Spirit; my Spirit is truth; truth abideth and hath no end; and if it be in you it shall abound." Note "if it be IN you" — spatial language, appropriate to substance. CAUTION Do not let this collapse into a crude physicalism. Draper's point is that the categories of spirit and matter are not opposites in Restoration thought, not that grace is a fluid. KEY TERMS • Material monism — the position that only one kind of stuff exists, and it is material. Joseph Smith's version does not say spirit is unreal; it says spirit is matter of a finer kind. "There is no such thing as immaterial matter." • Immaterial — without physical substance. Classical theology holds that God and spirit are immaterial; D&C 131:7 denies the category outright. • "More fine or pure" (131:7) — refined, subtle, rarefied. Not less real. More real, if anything. • "Purer eyes" (131:8) — perception refined enough to detect it. Note this is itself a capacity claim, which is the talk's theme showing up again. • Impartation — the act of giving a share of something. Draper's word for what happens when the Spirit is bestowed. WHY THIS MATTERS BEYOND THE TALK This is the doctrinal root of several distinctive Restoration positions: that God has a body, that spirit and element joined receive a fulness of joy (D&C 93:33), that resurrection is the reuniting of two material things. Draper is standing on a foundation Joseph Smith laid deliberately. CROSS-REFERENCES • D&C 93:33 — "spirit and element, inseparably connected, receive a fulness of joy." Same section, same monism. • D&C 88:15 — "the spirit and the body are the soul of man." If your class studied section 88, this is the identical doctrine. • Abraham 3:18–19 — spirits are eternal, uncreated, and graded. Feeds directly into slide 13. THE CAUTION, RESTATED Draper is not saying grace is a fluid or that light is measured in liters. He is saying the spirit/matter partition that most of Christian thought assumes is not one the Restoration accepts. Keep the claim at that level.
The hardest passage · D&C 93:29–30

Two Meanings of “Intelligence”

The puzzle: “All truth is independent in that sphere in which God has placed it, to act for itself.” But knowledge cannot act. So “truth” must mean more than knowledge.

Abstract

Intelligence = “light and truth” (93:36). Mental acuity — the faculty by which existence is cognized.

Concrete

Intelligence = “the light of truth” (93:29). The uncreated substance from which spirits are made — which can act.

If intelligence has two senses, so may truth: alongside “knowledge of things as they are,” truth can denote the things themselves — existence itself. Then “all truth… to act for itself” makes sense.
This is the most technical stretch of the talk. Go slowly, and give the class permission to find it hard — Draper calls it "another baffling scripture" himself. THE PUZZLE (D&C 93:30) "All truth is independent in that sphere in which God has placed it, TO ACT FOR ITSELF, as all intelligence also; otherwise there is no existence." Draper's objection is fair and precise: knowledge cannot act. It would make sense if the verse said truth IMPELS righteous action — but that is not what it says. Truth itself acts. THE SOLUTION — TWO SENSES OF "INTELLIGENCE" • Abstract: intelligence = "light and truth" (93:36). Mental acuity; the faculty by which existence is known. • Concrete: intelligence = "the light of truth" (93:29), which "was not created or made, neither indeed can be" — the uncreated spiritual substance from which our spirits are made. Verse 30, he argues, uses the CONCRETE sense. Substance can act. Knowledge cannot. THE EXTENSION — TWO SENSES OF "TRUTH" If intelligence has two senses, truth may too. Alongside "knowledge of things as they are," truth can denote the things themselves — existence. Then "all truth is independent… to act for itself" means everything that exists has a measure of independent agency. And the verse's closing clause follows naturally: "otherwise there is no existence." HIS SUMMARY "'All intelligence' identifies a component of the spirit aspect of existence. The phrase 'all truth' defines the whole of that existence." IF THE CLASS IS LOST Give them the one takeaway and move on: agency is not a privilege granted to humans; it goes all the way down, built into the fabric of what exists. That is enough. The technical exegesis is for the second reading. KEY TERMS • Intelligence, sense 1 (abstract) — "light and truth" (93:36). Mental acuity; the faculty by which existence is known. • Intelligence, sense 2 (concrete) — "the light of truth" (93:29). The uncreated spiritual substance from which spirits are made. Note the different phrasing: "light AND truth" versus "the light OF truth." Draper's whole distinction rests on that one preposition, so read both phrases aloud and let the class hear the difference. • Uncreated — without beginning; "was not created or made, neither indeed can be" (93:29). Co-eternal with God, not brought into being by Him. This is a genuinely distinctive Restoration claim. • Sphere — the assigned domain or bounds within which a thing is free. Independence here is bounded independence, not autonomy. • Cognize / cognition — to know or apprehend. Draper's term for what the abstract sense of intelligence does. WHY THE DISTINCTION IS NOT A DODGE A class member may reasonably suspect Draper invented a second meaning to escape a hard verse. He did not: verse 29 supplies the phrase "the light of truth" as a name for uncreated substance, and verse 36 supplies "light and truth" as a definition of glory. Both phrases are in the text. He is distinguishing two things scripture already distinguishes. CROSS-REFERENCES • Abraham 3:18–23 — intelligences as eternal, graded beings; "these two facts do exist, that there are two spirits." • D&C 93:33 — "man is spirit. The elements are eternal." • 2 Nephi 2:14–16 — things to act and things to be acted upon. This is the clearest scriptural statement of the acting/acted-upon distinction underlying verse 30, and it is a better on-ramp for most classes than verse 30 itself. If people are lost, teach 2 Nephi 2 first and come back.
D&C 93:31–32 · Where agency operates

They Receive Not the Light

“Behold, here is the agency of man, and here is the condemnation of man; because that which was from the beginning is plainly manifest unto them, and they receive not the light. And every man whose spirit receiveth not the light is under condemnation.”
— D&C 93:31–32
“Intelligence is free to choose or reject light. When it willfully rejects light, it also rejects truth, and condemnation follows.”
This is where the whole architecture gets its hinge. Everything upstream is given; everything downstream follows. The one place a human decision enters is here. THE VERSES "Here is the agency of man, and here is the condemnation of man; because that which was from the beginning is plainly manifest unto them, and they receive not the light." Note what is NOT the failure. Truth is "plainly manifest" — the problem is not that God hid anything. The failure is refusing the light, which is the capacity to take in what is already plainly there. THE MECHANISM OF CONDEMNATION Refusing light is not merely declining an upgrade. Because light is what makes truth receivable, refusing light forecloses truth — and truth is what glorifies. So the refusal is self-executing. Condemnation here is less a sentence imposed than a consequence entailed. CONNECT BACK TO D&C 88 IF YOU TAUGHT IT This is the same doctrine as D&C 88:32–33, the gift refused: "they were not willing to enjoy that which they might have received… what doth it profit a man if a gift is bestowed upon him, and he receive not the gift?" Same structure — universal offer, particular refusal, self-inflicted loss. KEY TERM • Condemnation — in this usage, being brought under judgment; the state of one who has foreclosed his own progress, rather than a punishment applied from outside.
John 14:6 · D&C 132:11–12

Only Through Him — and by Law

“I am the way, the truth, and the life: no man cometh unto the Father, but by me.”
— John 14:6
And the reason: He appoints nothing unto man “except it be by law, even as I and my Father ordained unto you, before the world was” — “no man shall come unto the Father but by me or by my word, which is my law.”
His word is Spirit, light, and truth. To receive the word is to receive light and truth.
Having built the machinery, Draper now names its source. This slide keeps the talk Christ-centered rather than abstract, and that matters — an argument this conceptual can drift. THE CLAIM John 14:6, "I am the way, the truth, and the life: no man cometh unto the Father, but by me." Note that Christ names Himself THE TRUTH — which, given everything we have established, is not a modest claim. THE REASON (D&C 132:11–12) He appoints nothing unto man "except it be by law, even as I and my Father ordained unto you, before the world was." And: "no man shall come unto the Father but by me or by my word, which is my law." THE LINK THAT CLOSES THE CIRCUIT We already established (D&C 84:45) that His word is equated with Spirit, light, and truth. Therefore receiving the word IS receiving light and truth. The exclusivity of Christ is not an arbitrary gatekeeping arrangement; He is the medium itself. THE PATTERN CLAIM "The Savior's objective is to bring obedient souls to a fulness of glory. He knows how, for He followed the way set down by the Father. And if man receives glory, it will be in the same way through which Christ received it." That sets up the next two slides, which are the heart of the talk. KEY TERMS • "The way, the truth, and the life" — three self-designations in one breath. Given what we have established, "the truth" is not a modest claim: if truth is the sum of existence, Christ is identifying Himself with it. • Ordained (D&C 132:11) — appointed, decreed, established beforehand. Not the priesthood sense here. • "My word, which is my law" — the equation that closes the circuit. His word is law; His word is also Spirit, light, and truth (84:45). So law and light are not two systems. • Expedient — see also D&C 88:64–65 if your class studied section 88; the same word governs what God grants. THE CONTEXT OF D&C 132 — HANDLE BRIEFLY Section 132 is primarily about eternal marriage and, in its later verses, plural marriage. Draper draws only on verses 11–12, which state a general principle: God appoints nothing except by law. If someone notices the source and is distracted by it, name it plainly, note that the cited principle is general rather than specific to the section's main subject, and move on. Do not pretend the section is about something else. WHY THIS SLIDE MATTERS STRUCTURALLY An argument this abstract can drift into philosophy. This slide re-anchors it in a Person. Everything described so far — grace, light, capacity, truth — has a single source and a single channel, and He is not a mechanism. CROSS-REFERENCES • John 1:16 — "of his fulness have all we received, and grace for grace." The phrase Draper builds on in two slides comes from John, and D&C 93 is explicitly presented as the testimony of John. • D&C 88:6–13 — the light-of-Christ passage that opened the talk.
D&C 93:12–13 · Two distinct motions

Grace for Grace · Grace to Grace

“He received not of the fulness at the first, but received grace for grace; …but continued from grace to grace, until he received a fulness.”
— D&C 93:12–13

Grace for grace

An exchange. “To receive assistance on the condition of giving assistance.” The favor received obligates the receiver to extend it.

From grace to grace

An ascent. The repeated exchange compounds — “until he received a fulness.”

Draper does something exegetically careful here that most readers skate past: he treats the two phrases as naming two DIFFERENT motions, not as poetic repetition. THE TWO MOTIONS • "Grace for grace" — reciprocal exchange. You receive on condition of giving. "Thus the extension of favor is meant to obligate the recipient so that she or he will extend the same. As they meet this condition, more grace is extended to them, which further obligates them to greater assistance to others." • "From grace to grace" — progressive ascent. The repeated exchange produces increase until fulness. HIS DEFINITION OF GRACE — NOTE THE PRECISION The word denotes favor, kindness, goodwill; theologically, "the free unmerited love and favor of God." But then he specifies what makes assistance into GRACE rather than mere help: "the kindness and favor felt by the giver that is extended to the receiver WHEN SUCH SERVICE MAY NOT EVEN BE DESERVED." So grace = help + disposition + undeservedness. All three. Help given grudgingly is not grace; help given to the deserving is closer to wages. THE ENGINE This is the loop that drives the entire ascent: receive grace → extend grace → receive more grace → extend more. It is not a ladder you climb alone. It is a circuit, and other people are in it. DISCUSSION Ask: if grace received obligates me to extend it, who is currently owed grace by me? KEY TERMS • Grace — favor, kindness, goodwill; theologically, "the free unmerited love and favor of God." The Greek behind the New Testament term is charis, which also gives us "charity" by way of Latin caritas, and "eucharist" (thanksgiving). The word family is about gift. • Unmerited — undeserved. Draper insists this is essential: assistance rendered to someone who has earned it is closer to wages than to grace. • Reciprocity — mutual exchange; each party giving in return. Draper's key structural claim about "grace for grace." • Obligate — to bind by duty. Note this is a strong word, and Draper chose it deliberately: grace received creates a genuine debt, discharged by extending grace onward rather than by repaying the giver. • Fulness — the complete measure, the destination of the process (93:13). THE SOURCE OF THE PHRASE — DRAPER DOESN'T NAME IT "Grace for grace" is not original to section 93. It is John 1:16: "And of his fulness have all we received, and grace for grace." Section 93 opens by presenting itself as the fuller testimony of John, so the borrowing is deliberate. Pointing this out strengthens the exposition considerably — the phrase carries a New Testament pedigree. A HONEST NOTE ON THE READING Most biblical commentators read John 1:16's "grace for grace" as "grace upon grace" — grace heaped on grace, an intensification rather than an exchange. Draper's reciprocal reading (receive by giving) is a legitimate reading of the D&C usage, especially given verse 20's "if you keep my commandments… therefore you shall receive grace for grace." But it is a reading, not the only one. If you have someone in the class who has studied John, acknowledge that. CROSS-REFERENCE Matthew 10:8 — "freely ye have received, freely give." The same logic in one line, and an easier entry point for most classes.
The talk’s most arresting sentence

“In a very real way, Christ Himself was saved by grace.”

He received grace from the Father, extended it to His brethren, and received more — “until He eventually received a fulness of the glory of the Father.”

The path is not a lesser accommodation for weak people. It is the road the Savior walked, offered on the same terms.
Let this one sit before you explain it. It is the sentence people will remember, and it will startle some of them. WHY IT IS NOT HERESY It follows necessarily from D&C 93:12–13, which Draper has just read. If Christ "received not of the fulness at the first" and progressed by receiving and extending grace until He received a fulness, then the mechanism of His glorification is the same mechanism as ours. Draper is not diminishing Christ; he is taking seriously what section 93 actually says about His progression. THE CAREFUL QUALIFIER Note "in a very real way" — Draper is not claiming Christ needed redemption from sin. He is claiming Christ advanced by the grace-for-grace process. The parallel is about the METHOD of glorification, not about a need for forgiveness. THE VERSE THAT MAKES THE PARALLEL EXPLICIT D&C 93:20 — "if you keep my commandments you shall receive of his fulness, and be glorified in me as I am in the Father; therefore, I say unto you, you shall receive grace for grace." The same phrase applied to Christ in verse 12 is applied to us in verse 20. That repetition is deliberate. THE PASTORAL FORCE This is the emotional center of the talk. The way of grace is not a concession designed for people who could not manage holiness on their own. It is the actual road the Savior walked, and we are invited onto it on identical terms. KEY TERMS • Progression — incremental advancement toward a fulness. Applied to Christ in 93:12–13, and to us in 93:20. • "Received not of the fulness at the first" — the phrase that makes the claim possible. Whatever else is true of Christ's mortal life, section 93 says He began without the fulness and grew into it. • Saved — used here in the sense of "brought to a fulness of glory," not "rescued from sin." Draper's qualifier "in a very real way" is carrying that distinction; make it explicit so nobody hears something he did not say. HANDLE THIS CAREFULLY Someone may hear "Christ was saved by grace" as implying Christ needed forgiveness. He did not, and Draper is not saying so. The claim is strictly about the METHOD of glorification: He advanced by receiving and extending grace. Sinlessness and the need to progress are different questions, and section 93 asserts the second without touching the first. THE STRONGEST SUPPORT — DRAPER DOESN'T CITE IT Luke 2:52: "And Jesus increased in wisdom and stature, and in favour with God and man." The King James "favour" is precisely the word grace translates. That verse states Christ's progression in grace in the plainest possible terms, and it is in the Gospels rather than only in Restoration scripture — which makes it a useful bridge for anyone uneasy with the claim. CROSS-REFERENCES • D&C 93:12–14, 16–17 — the full progression passage, ending "he received all power, both in heaven and on earth." • Hebrews 5:8–9 — "though he were a Son, yet learned he obedience by the things which he suffered; and being made perfect…" The same doctrine in Paul's vocabulary. • JST John 1:16 and D&C 93:20 — the promise extended to us in the identical phrase.
The surprising claim

You Cannot Reject Grace

“The agency of man is expressed in accepting or rejecting light. But he is not free to accept or reject grace. Grace comes to all men freely, as it is the unmerited favor that God holds for all His children.”
— Draper, summary

Not on the table

Grace. God’s standing disposition toward every one of His children. It is not offered for approval.

On the table

Light. What grace delivers. This — and only this — is where agency operates.

This is the claim I would build a lesson around, because it quietly inverts how we usually talk. THE COMMON FRAMING In ordinary devotional usage, grace is something you "access," "qualify for," "receive through faith," or "apply in your life." Grace is treated as conditional and as something we opt into. DRAPER'S FRAMING Grace is upstream of human choice entirely. It is God's standing disposition toward all His children, and it is not on the table. "Grace allows light to flow unto each person. Thus light, through grace, is freely manifest unto all." WHAT IS ACTUALLY CHOSEN The light that grace carries. That is the only decision point in the whole architecture — which is exactly what D&C 93:31–32 said two slides ago: "here is the agency of man… they receive not the light." THE CONSEQUENCE HE DRAWS "When people reject light, they reject God's favor and cut themselves off from truth. Thus they stand under condemnation." You cannot decline grace. You can only decline what it delivers — and in declining the delivery you have functionally declined the favor. WHY THIS IS GOOD NEWS Nobody in your class is outside the reach of grace, and nobody has to earn their way into its range. It is already there, on all of them, right now. The only question is a much smaller and more manageable one: will I take what it is carrying today? KEY TERMS • Unmerited favor — the standard theological definition of grace, and the reason Draper says it cannot be conditional: a favor you had to qualify for would not be unmerited. • Prevenient grace — the historic Christian term (Augustine, and later central to Wesley and Methodism) for grace that GOES BEFORE human choice and makes a free response possible. Draper never uses the phrase, but his position is close to it, and naming it helps a class see he is not inventing something eccentric — he is arriving at a well-trodden idea by a Restoration route. • Condemnation — being brought under judgment; here, the self-inflicted state of one who has foreclosed his own progress. • Agency — the capacity to choose. Draper's precision is that agency has a specific object in this architecture. It is not a general power to accept or refuse God's love; it is the power to accept or refuse the light that love delivers. THE DISTINCTION THAT MAKES IT WORK Grace is God's DISPOSITION — what He is toward His children. Light is the DELIVERY — what that disposition sends. You cannot alter someone else's disposition toward you by refusing their gift; you can only fail to receive the gift. That is why Draper can say rejecting light is rejecting God's favor while still insisting grace itself is not refusable. CROSS-REFERENCES • Matthew 5:45 — He "maketh his sun to rise on the evil and on the good." Sunlight is the perfect image for this slide: universally shed, and you can still stay indoors. • Moroni 7:16 — "the Spirit of Christ is given to every man, that he may know good from evil." Universal bestowal, stated flatly. • 2 Nephi 26:24, 33 — "he doeth not anything save it be for the benefit of the world… all are alike unto God."

Reading honestly

A Seam in the Argument

Section 6 says

“First comes obedience to the word, then light, and finally truth.” Obedience precedes light.

The summary says

“Light, through grace, is freely manifest unto all.” Light comes before any obedience.

The reconciliation: light is offered freely to everyone (D&C 84:46) — that is grace. Light is retained and increased through obedience: “he that receiveth light, and continueth in God, receiveth more light.”
— D&C 50:24
The first portion is given. Every portion after that is a response.
I have included this slide because a careful reader in your class may notice the tension, and it is better to name it than to have it noticed and left hanging. Drop the slide if your audience is not that kind of room. THE TENSION • In the obedience section, Draper writes: "But first comes obedience to the word, then light, and finally truth." Obedience precedes light. • In the summary he writes: "Grace comes to all men freely… Grace allows light to flow unto each person. Thus light, through grace, is freely manifest unto all." Light precedes obedience, and agency consists in accepting or rejecting it. These cannot both be the whole story as stated. THE RECONCILIATION They describe different STAGES of one process. • Light is OFFERED to everyone freely, prior to any obedience. That is grace, and D&C 84:46 states it: "the Spirit giveth light to every man that cometh into the world." • Light is RETAINED AND INCREASED through obedience. D&C 50:24 puts the condition in the middle of the sentence: "he that receiveth light, AND CONTINUETH IN GOD, receiveth more light." So obedience is not the price of admission. It is the condition of continuance. The first portion is given; every portion after that is a response. WHY SAY THIS OUT LOUD Because it protects the doctrine on both sides. It keeps grace genuinely free — nobody has to earn their way into God's favor — while keeping obedience genuinely necessary. Naming the seam makes the talk stronger, not weaker, and it models honest reading for your class. KEY TERMS • "Continueth in God" (D&C 50:24) — the conditional clause sitting in the middle of the verse, and the whole hinge of the reconciliation. He that receiveth light AND CONTINUETH receiveth more. The first portion is unconditioned; the increase is conditioned. • Manifest — made evident, disclosed. In the summary, light is "freely manifest unto all" — offered openly, not privately awarded. • Exegesis — drawing meaning out of a text. Eisegesis is the opposite error: reading your meaning into it. Naming a seam rather than smoothing it is what keeps exposition on the exegesis side. • Condition of continuance — a useful phrase for the class: obedience is not the price of admission, it is the condition of continuance. THE TWO-STAGE MODEL, STATED CLEANLY Stage 1 — the initial portion of light: given to everyone, unconditionally, by grace. D&C 84:46, "the Spirit giveth light to EVERY MAN that cometh into the world." Stage 2 — increase: conditioned on response. D&C 84:46 again, in its second half, gives enlightenment specifically to "every man… THAT HEARKENETH." The verse itself contains both stages, which is why it is the best single proof text for the reconciliation. WHY I WOULD NOT SKIP THIS SLIDE IN A CLOSE-READING CLASS Devotional exposition often smooths over inconsistencies to protect a speaker. That teaches people that faithful reading means not noticing things. Showing a class that you can spot a genuine tension, name it, and resolve it from the text is worth more than the doctrinal content of the slide. BUT DO SKIP IT IF Your class is new, or the hour is short, or the room needs the talk's comfort more than its rigor. Nothing downstream depends on this slide.

Grace — what delivers capacity and what we pass on. His subtitle called them "interrelated," and by now the class should be able to say exactly how: each one is the condition of the next.
