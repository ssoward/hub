# Analysis: Doctrine and Covenants 88 — "The Olive Leaf"
**Revelation given through Joseph Smith** | *Kirtland, Ohio · December 27–28, 1832 and January 3, 1833*

> "The 'olive leaf' … plucked from the Tree of Paradise, the Lord's message of peace to us."
> — Joseph Smith's own designation for the section

---

## Textual Profile

| Measure | Value |

The frequency table is not trivia. It maps the section's obsessions: **light**, **law**, **kingdom**, **all things**, **house**. Those five words are the section's skeleton.

---

## Historical Frame

The revelation was received at a conference of high priests in Kirtland, three days after Section 87 — the **prophecy on war**, which named South Carolina and forecast the pouring out of war upon all nations. Section 88 is deliberately positioned as the answer to Section 87.

The title is the interpretive key. In Genesis 8:11, Noah's dove returns with an olive leaf: proof that the waters of judgment are receding and that living things exist again. Joseph Smith is claiming that this revelation performs the same office.

**But note the paradox that governs everything below.** Section 88 contains the most extended apocalyptic material in the Doctrine and Covenants — earthquakes, a moon bathed in blood, seven angels, seven trumps, the battle of Michael. It is not a peaceful text in the sense of being a calm one. Its peace is not the absence of catastrophe. Its peace is **light, law, and a house of God located inside the catastrophe**. Any reading that softens the apocalypse to preserve the "olive leaf" title has inverted the section's logic.

## Structure: Five Movements

| | Verses | Movement | Governing Question |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | 1–5 | The Comforter promised | What is being given? |
| II | 6–50 | Light, law, and kingdoms | How does reality work? |
| III | 51–61 | The parable of the twelve kingdoms | How does God attend to it all? |
| IV | 62–116 | Seek, sanctify, teach — and the end | What is asked of us, and where is history going? |
| V | 117–141 | Establish a house | What do we build now? |

The most striking structural fact: the section travels from **"the immensity of space"** (v. 12) to **what time to go to bed** (v. 124). Cosmology descends into domesticity — and the descent is not a change of subject. It is the same principle applied at both scales. Law governs planetary courses (v. 43) and sleep schedules (v. 124), and both are sanctified the same way: by abiding it.

## Primary Points

### 1. Light is not a metaphor in this section — it is an ontology.
Verses 6–13 make a claim without close parallel in scripture. The light of Christ is *simultaneously*:

- the light **of the sun**, and "the power thereof by which it was made" (v. 7)
- the light of the moon and stars (vv. 8–9)
- the light "which quickeneth your understandings" (v. 11)
- that which "proceedeth forth from the presence of God to fill the immensity of space" (v. 12)
- "the light which is in all things, which giveth life to all things, **which is the law by which all things are governed**" (v. 13)

Verse 13 is the hinge of the entire section. In one sentence, light is identified with **physics** (what makes stars burn), **biology** (what gives life), **jurisprudence** (the law by which all things are governed), **cognition** (what quickens understanding), and **person** (the power of God who sits on his throne). Restoration theology here refuses the partition between natural law and moral law. They are one substance, and that substance is a Person.

### 2. "Comprehend" is the section's most loaded verb, used in both of its senses.
The word appears seven times across six verses, and the section plays the two meanings — *to understand* and *to encompass/contain* — against each other:

- v. 6 — Christ "descended below all things, in that he **comprehended** all things" (*encompass* — and note the causal claim: descent is the precondition of encompassing)
- v. 41 — "He **comprehendeth** all things, and all things are before him… and round about him" (*encompass*)
- v. 43 — "the courses of the heavens and the earth, which **comprehend** the earth and all the planets" (*contain*)
- v. 48–49 — "he who came unto his own was not **comprehended**… the darkness **comprehendeth** it not" (*understand*)
- v. 49 — "the day shall come when you shall **comprehend** even God" (*understand*)
- v. 67 — "that body which is filled with light **comprehendeth** all things" (*both*)

The doctrinal payload: **Christ comprehends by descending. We comprehend by being filled.** Verse 67 collapses the two senses — the body filled with light both contains and understands. Comprehension is not primarily an intellectual achievement; it is a capacity produced by saturation.

### 3. Law is the condition of existence, not a restriction upon it.
Verse 34 is the thesis: "that which is governed by law is also **preserved** by law and **perfected** and **sanctified** by the same." Law is not the fence around the field; it is the soil.

Verses 36–38 push this to its limit: "there is **no space** in the which there is no kingdom; and there is **no kingdom** in which there is no space." Space and kingdom are co-extensive. There is no lawless territory anywhere in the universe.

The counter-case in verse 35 is therefore devastating: that which "seeketh to become **a law unto itself**… cannot be sanctified by law, neither by mercy, justice, nor judgment." Note that mercy is on that list. Radical autonomy does not merely earn punishment — it renders the being *unsanctifiable*, because it has stepped outside the only mechanism by which sanctification operates.

### 4. Judgment is described as capacity, not verdict.
Verses 22–24 use one verb, repeated: **abide**.

> "He who is not **able to abide** the law of a celestial kingdom cannot abide a celestial glory."

The grammar is diagnostic, not punitive. Nobody is expelled from celestial glory; some are unable to remain in it. This is reinforced by verse 40's **law of affinity** — "intelligence cleaveth unto intelligence; wisdom receiveth wisdom; truth embraceth truth; virtue loveth virtue; light cleaveth unto light." Sorting is **self**-sorting. Like gathers to like by nature, not by sentence.

And it is reinforced again in verses 28–31: you receive "a fulness" of the glory **by which you are already quickened**. Future glory is the maturation of present spiritual condition, not a reassignment to it.

### 5. The gift refused (vv. 32–33) is the section's most merciful and most sobering passage.
Of those who receive no glory: "they shall return again to their own place, to enjoy that which they are **willing** to receive, because they were **not willing** to enjoy that which they might have received."

Then verse 33, which reads like a sigh:

> "For what doth it profit a man if a gift is bestowed upon him, and he **receive not** the gift? Behold, he rejoices not in that which is given unto him, neither rejoices in **him who is the giver** of the gift."

The bestowal is universal. The reception is not. And the second half of verse 33 identifies the real loss — not the unopened gift, but the unknown Giver.

### 6. The soul is spirit *and* body (vv. 15–16).
"The spirit and the body are the soul of man. And the resurrection from the dead is the redemption of the soul." Eight words dismantle the Platonic and creedal inheritance in which the body is the soul's prison. Here, a disembodied spirit is not a liberated soul — it is an incomplete one. Salvation is necessarily embodied, and resurrection is not an appendix to redemption but its completion.

### 7. The earth is a covenant-keeping being (vv. 17–20, 25–26).
"The earth **abideth the law of a celestial kingdom**, for it **filleth the measure of its creation**, and transgresseth not the law." The earth will die, be quickened, be sanctified, and be inherited by "the poor and the meek."

The rhetorical function is comparative. The earth is held up as the one creation in the passage that is already doing what the Saints are being asked to do. And the standard it meets is stated in a phrase worth memorizing: **filling the measure of its creation** — not exceeding one's design, and not falling short of it.

## Secondary Points

### The parable of the twelve kingdoms (vv. 51–61) has no biblical parallel.
A lord sends twelve servants into a field. He visits each one — the first in the first hour, the second in the second, on to the twelfth — and **tarries with each the full hour**: "he was made glad with the light of the countenance of his lord."

Three readings, all supported by the text:

1. **Cosmological** — verse 61 makes it explicit: "unto this parable I will liken all these kingdoms." Worlds without number, each receiving its dispensation in its season.
2. **Historical** — the sequence of dispensations, "every kingdom in its hour, and in its time, and in its season."
3. **Pastoral** — the reading most useful in a class. God's attention is not divided among his children; it is **sequential and total**. The lord withdrawing from the first servant is not abandonment; it is the same undivided attention moving to the second. And verse 59 forecloses despair about position in the queue: "beginning at the first, and so on unto the last, and from the last unto the first, and from the first unto the last." The circuit runs both directions. No last is finally last.

Verse 60 supplies the mutual telos: "that his lord might be glorified **in him**, and he **in his lord**, that they all might be glorified." Glory here is reciprocal, not conferred downward.

### Revelation is reciprocal and conditional (vv. 62–68).
The chain of conditionals is unusually dense: *Draw near → I will draw near. Seek diligently → ye shall find. Ask → ye shall receive. Knock → it shall be opened.* Verse 67 supplies the aperture: "if your **eye be single** to my glory, your whole bodies shall be filled with light, and there shall be no darkness in you."

Verse 65 is the honest counterweight most treatments omit: "if ye ask anything that is **not expedient** for you, it shall turn unto your **condemnation**." Unanswered prayer is not the only risk in prayer. Answered prayer can be.

### The curriculum in verses 76–80 is astonishingly broad.
"Teach ye diligently and **my grace shall attend you**" — grace is promised to attend an act of *teaching*, which is itself a claim worth pausing on. Then the syllabus:

> "Things both in heaven and in the earth, and under the earth; things which have been, things which are, things which must shortly come to pass; things which are at home, things which are abroad; the wars and the perplexities of the nations, and the judgments which are on the land; and a knowledge also of countries and of kingdoms."

Astronomy, geology, history, current events, geopolitics, geography. The stated motive in verse 80 is not enrichment but readiness: "**that ye may be prepared in all things** when I shall send you again."

Verse 118 supplies the method — and its framing is easy to miss: "**And as all have not faith**, seek ye diligently and teach one another words of wisdom; yea, seek ye out of the **best books** words of wisdom; seek learning, even by **study** and also by **faith**." Study is introduced as the accommodation to a community of uneven faith. It is not presented as the lesser path, but it is presented as the accessible one — the door that is always open.

### The apocalyptic center (vv. 87–116) is organized as seven trumps over seven millennia.

| Trump | Verses | What sounds |
|---|---|---|
| First | 96–98 | The celestial dead and living — "they are Christ's, the first fruits" — caught up to meet him |
| Second | 99 | Redemption of those who received the gospel in spirit prison |
| Third | 100–101 | Those under condemnation; "they live not again until the thousand years are ended" |
| Fourth | 102 | Those who "shall remain filthy still" |
| Fifth | 103–104 | The everlasting gospel to all nations — "every knee shall bow, and every tongue shall confess" |
| Sixth | 105 | "She is fallen, is fallen!" |
| Seventh | 106 | "It is finished! The Lamb of God hath overcome and trodden the wine-press alone" |

Then the trumps sound a **second time** (vv. 108–110), each revealing "the secret acts of men, and the thoughts and intents of their hearts, and the mighty works of God" for its thousand years. History is read back aloud, in full, in order. This is a striking model of final judgment: not a sentencing, but a **disclosure**.

Verse 88 sets the causal order, and it is not the order most expect: "**after your testimony** cometh wrath and indignation upon the people." Testimony precedes judgment. Verse 81 draws the practical consequence: "it becometh every man who hath been warned to **warn his neighbor**."

### Verse 119 is the section's architectural summary.
> "Organize yourselves; prepare every needful thing; and establish a house, even a house of **prayer**, a house of **fasting**, a house of **faith**, a house of **learning**, a house of **glory**, a house of **order**, a house of **God**."

Seven descriptors, and the word "house" eight times in a single verse. Two observations rarely made:

1. **Learning sits at the center** — fourth of seven, flanked by faith and glory. The house of God is a school. Verses 127–141 make this literal: the section closes by legislating the School of the Prophets.
2. The verse was given three and a half years before the Kirtland Temple was dedicated. Section 88 is the commission; Sections 109–110 are the fulfillment. The section's whole trajectory is toward "the **great and last promise**" of verse 69 — "the days will come that you shall see him; for he will unveil his face unto you… in his own time, and in his own way, and according to his own will."

### The homely commandments (vv. 121–126) are the section's most surprising turn.
After the wine-press of the wrath of Almighty God comes this:

> "Cease to be idle; cease to be unclean; **cease to find fault one with another**; cease to sleep longer than is needful; **retire to thy bed early**, that ye may not be weary; **arise early**, that your bodies and your minds may be invigorated."

The tonal whiplash is the point. The same law that fixes the courses of the planets governs the hour you go to bed, and for the same stated reason — so that body and mind are fit for the work.

Verse 122 is a complete charter for how a class should be conducted, and deserves to be read aloud in any teacher council: "let **not all be spokesmen at once**; but let **one speak at a time** and let **all listen** unto his sayings, that when all have spoken **that all may be edified of all**, and that every man may have an **equal privilege**."

Verse 125 caps the ethic: "and above all things, **clothe yourselves with the bond of charity, as with a mantle**, which is the bond of perfectness and peace." The mantle is an *outer* garment — worn over everything else, and the thing others see first.

## Tertiary Points

1. **"The alms of your prayers" (v. 2)** — an arresting compound. Prayers are figured as charitable offerings that "come up into the ears of the Lord of Sabaoth," recorded in "the book of the names of the sanctified." Prayer is an act of giving, not only of asking.
2. **The Comforter of verse 3** is distinguished from the Holy Ghost — "another Comforter… the Holy Spirit of promise," identified with "the promise… of eternal life" (v. 4). This links the section to John 14 and to the doctrine of the Second Comforter.
3. **"Quicken" appears eleven times** and carries the section's dynamism — the earth is quickened (v. 26), bodies are quickened by a portion of glory (vv. 28–31), the saints are quickened and caught up (v. 96), and we shall "comprehend even God, being **quickened in him and by him**" (v. 49).
4. **Verse 41's eightfold "all things"** is the section's rhetorical high-water mark — before, above, in, through, round about, by, and of. The syntax performs the doctrine: the sentence encircles the reader the way God encircles all things.
5. **Verse 45's "The earth rolls upon her wings"** — one of the most beautiful cosmological images in the standard works, and notable for its feminine pronouns for earth and moon.
6. **Verse 47 makes an epistemological claim of unusual generosity**: "any man who hath seen any or the least of these hath **seen God** moving in his majesty and power." A glimpse of the least kingdom is a genuine sight of God. Verse 48 immediately supplies the qualification — seeing is not comprehending.
7. **Verse 86's "Abide ye in the liberty wherewith ye are made free"** places freedom and law in the same sentence without apology, consistent with the section's whole argument.
8. **The salutation of verse 133** is a covenant of friendship with remarkable language: "in a determination that is **fixed, immovable, and unchangeable**, to be your friend and brother through the grace of God in the bonds of love." Friendship is here made a formal ordinance of the school.
9. **The washing of feet (vv. 139–141)** closes the section by pointing directly back to John 13, tying the School of the Prophets to the Last Supper — instruction framed as discipleship, not merely curriculum.
10. **Verse 69's "cast away your idle thoughts and your excess of laughter"** and verse 121's "cease from all your light speeches" are not asceticism for its own sake. Both sit immediately adjacent to promises of seeing God's face. The counsel is about *aperture* — the same principle as the single eye.

## Literary Devices

- **Anaphora** — "as also he is in the sun… as also he is in the moon… as also the light of the stars" (vv. 7–9); "and another angel shall sound his trump" (vv. 99–106); "cease… cease… cease" (vv. 123–124).
- **Chiasm of sequence** — "beginning at the first, and so on unto the last, and from the last unto the first, and from the first unto the last" (v. 59).
- **Semantic pivot** — the two senses of "comprehend," turned deliberately across vv. 6, 41, 48–49, and 67.
- **Scalar descent** — the deliberate movement from the immensity of space (v. 12) to bedtime (v. 124), enacting the claim that one law governs both.
- **Inclusio of light** — the section opens with light filling immensity (v. 12) and returns to light filling a body (v. 67); the cosmic aperture narrows to a personal one.
- **Extended parable** — vv. 51–61, unique to the Restoration.
- **Apocalyptic catalogue** — the seven trumps (vv. 99–110), sounded twice, the second pass converting judgment into disclosure.

## Teaching Applications

| Audience | Entry point |
|---|---|
| **Sunday School / Come, Follow Me** | Verses 63 and 118 — the reciprocity of seeking, and study *and* faith as complementary. Verse 122 as the rule for the discussion itself. |
| **Teacher council** | Verses 77–80, 118, 122. Grace attends teaching; the curriculum is broad; everyone gets equal privilege to speak. |
| **Youth** | Verses 32–33 (the gift refused) and 40 (light cleaves to light). Concrete, non-abstract, and immediately applicable to what one chooses to be near. |
| **Adults in hard seasons** | Verses 51–61. The lord tarries the full hour with each servant. If it is not your hour yet, it is coming. |
| **Temple preparation** | Verses 68–69, 119. The "great and last promise," and the seven-fold house — the section is the Kirtland Temple's charter. |
| **Personal study** | Verses 6–13 and 67, read together. Descent, saturation, comprehension. |

## The Governing Thesis

Section 88 answers Section 87's prophecy of war not by denying the war, but by revealing what holds while it happens.

**Light fills all space. Law governs every kingdom. And in the middle of the earthquakes and the trumps, the Saints are told to organize themselves, establish a house of learning and order and glory, teach one another, speak one at a time, go to bed early, and put on charity like a coat.**

That is the olive leaf. Not the announcement that the flood is over — the evidence that something is alive inside it, and the instructions for building on the ground as it dries.
