# Analysis: "Light, Truth, and Grace — Three Interrelated Principles Necessary for Exaltation"
**Richard D. Draper** | BYU Devotional, July 7, 2009 | Professor of Ancient Scripture, BYU

---

## The Talk in One Sentence

Grace flows universally and unconditionally to all people; it carries **light**, which is not information but *capacity*; capacity is what makes **truth** receivable; and truth — not light — is what glorifies. Agency operates at exactly one point in that chain: whether we accept the light.

## Primary Points

### 1. Light is capacity, not content. This is the talk's central contribution.
Nearly every treatment of "light and truth" collapses the two into synonyms for *knowledge*. Draper's whole argument depends on prying them apart:

> "Truth defined in this way is always associated with light because truth can only be acquired through the power or the capacitating force of light. Without the faculty created by light, a fulness of truth could never be gained."

His definition, arrived at inductively from D&C 88:11–13, is **"living and capacitating energy."** Light is not the thing known. Light is the enlargement of the knower that makes knowing possible.

The **pint-and-quart analogy** is the talk's most portable teaching device, and it is doing real conceptual work:

> "If my spiritual mind is pint-sized, I can only hold a pint of truth. If God is to give me more, the capacity of my mind must be expanded. Light does the expansion."

The pastoral payoff is significant. If light were information, spiritual growth would be an accumulation problem — read more, learn more. If light is capacity, spiritual growth is a *container* problem, and the container is enlarged by God rather than by effort. This reframes discouragement: the person who feels they cannot grasp the gospel is not failing to study hard enough; they are describing a capacity that God has undertaken to expand.

### 2. Glory is primarily radiance, not approbation — and the argument is lexical.
Draper's section 2 stakes everything on a dictionary. He notes that the Christian tradition — he names **Milton, Johnson, Aquinas, and C. S. Lewis** — read scriptural "glory" as *approval, honor, renown*. On that reading, "the glory of God" means the esteem God grants or receives.

He counters with the dictionary available to Joseph Smith. **This checks out, and more strongly than he claims.** In Webster's 1828, the noun *glory* is defined:

1. "Brightness; luster; splendor."
2. "Splendor; magnificence."
3. The halo of rays around a figure's head in art.
4. Praise and honor in worship.
5. Renown, fame, celebrity.

Draper says fame and praise are "secondary." They are in fact *fourth and fifth*. Radiance is not merely primary — it occupies the first three entries.

**Why the stakes are high:** if glory is approbation, then D&C 93:36 ("The glory of God is intelligence, or, in other words, light and truth") is nearly unintelligible — approval is not intelligence. If glory is radiance, and light is a constituent of glory, the verse becomes a definition rather than a riddle. The lexical move is what makes the rest of the talk possible.

### 3. Glory is a capacitating agent — the Moses evidence.
The strongest scriptural support for reading glory as *enabling power* rather than *conferred honor* is Moses 1:

> "The glory of God was upon Moses; **therefore Moses could endure his presence**." (Moses 1:2)

The word *therefore* is load-bearing. Glory is not the reward for the interview; it is the equipment that makes the interview survivable. Draper presses further with Moses 1:4–5: seeing all of God's works would require beholding all His glory, and no one can do that and "remain in the flesh." Glory is what gives God "the capacity to be all-seeing."

Note the structural rhyme: glory capacitates Moses to endure God's presence, exactly as light capacitates the mind to receive truth. Draper has found the same mechanism operating at two scales.

### 4. Light is literal substance, not metaphor.
This is where the talk becomes distinctively Restoration theology. Citing **D&C 131:7–8** ("all spirit is matter"), Draper concludes:

> "Its bestowal upon an individual is an impartation of **actual celestial substance** — actual elements of spirit producing higher power, higher capacity, higher life."

Most Christian theology treats light-language as figurative. Joseph Smith's material monism makes it literal: receiving the Spirit is receiving *stuff*. The capacity enlargement is physical in some real sense. Anyone teaching this talk should mark the moment — it is the point at which the argument stops being available to most of Christendom.

Supporting chain, **D&C 84:45**: "the word of the Lord is truth, and whatsoever is truth is light, and whatsoever is light is Spirit, even the Spirit of Jesus Christ." Word = truth = light = Spirit. Draper's gloss: "They are inseparably welded together so that a person cannot be touched by one without being touched by all."

### 5. Truth — not light — is the glorifying principle.
> "One is not, however, glorified in light… Exaltation is contingent upon the reception of the other, all-important element of which glory is composed, namely, truth." (cf. D&C 93:28)

Truth is defined at D&C 93:24 as "knowledge of things as they are, and as they were, and as they are to come." Draper glosses this with the hymn *Oh Say, What Is Truth?* — "truth is the sum of existence."

This ordering matters. Light is the means; truth is the end. A talk that stopped at light would leave you with capacity and nothing to put in it.

### 6. Grace is universal and unconditional — and agency does not apply to it.
The most counterintuitive claim in the talk, made almost in passing in the summary:

> "The agency of man is expressed in accepting or rejecting light. **But he is not free to accept or reject grace.** Grace comes to all men freely, as it is the unmerited favor that God holds for all His children. Grace allows light to flow unto each person."

This inverts common devotional usage, where grace is something one "accesses," "qualifies for," or "receives through faith." Draper places grace *upstream* of human choice entirely. Grace is the standing disposition of God toward all His children; it is not on the table. What is on the table is the light that grace carries.

The consequence he draws is sharp: "When people reject light, they reject God's favor and cut themselves off from truth." You cannot decline grace. You can only decline what it delivers.

### 7. "Grace for grace" is reciprocal; "from grace to grace" is progressive.
Draper reads D&C 93:12–13 as naming two distinct motions, and the distinction is exegetically careful:

- **Grace for grace** — an exchange. "To receive grace for grace is to receive assistance on the condition of giving assistance." The favor received obligates the recipient to extend it.
- **From grace to grace** — an ascent. The repeated exchange produces increase, "until he received a fulness."

His definition of grace is worth quoting for its precision about what distinguishes grace from mere help: "What transforms assistance into grace is the **kindness and favor felt by the giver** that is extended to the receiver **when such service may not even be deserved**." Grace is help plus disposition plus undeservedness.

### 8. "In a very real way, Christ Himself was saved by grace."
The talk's most arresting sentence, and it follows necessarily from what precedes it. If Christ "received not of the fulness at the first" (D&C 93:12) and progressed by receiving and extending grace, then the mechanism of His glorification is the mechanism of ours. D&C 93:20 makes the parallel explicit: "you shall receive of his fulness, and be glorified in me as I am in the Father; therefore… you shall receive grace for grace."

The pastoral force: the path is not a lesser accommodation designed for weak people. It is the actual path the Savior walked, offered on the same terms.

## Secondary Points

### The two definitions of "intelligence" (the hardest passage)
Draper's most technical exegesis solves a genuine puzzle in **D&C 93:30**: "All truth is independent in that sphere in which God has placed it, **to act for itself**, as all intelligence also."

His objection is fair: knowledge cannot *act*. So either the verse is loose, or "truth" means something more than knowledge here.

His solution distinguishes two senses of *intelligence*:
- **Abstract** — "light and truth" (D&C 93:36); mental acuity, the faculty by which existence is cognized.
- **Concrete** — "the light of truth" (D&C 93:29); the uncreated spiritual substance from which spirits are made.

Verse 30, he argues, uses the concrete sense. And if *intelligence* has two senses, so may *truth*: alongside "knowledge of things as they are," truth can denote **the things themselves** — existence itself. On that reading "all truth is independent… to act for itself" means *everything that exists has a measure of independent agency* — which is coherent, and which makes the verse's closing clause ("otherwise there is no existence") follow naturally.

This is the section most likely to lose a class. It is also the section that most repays a second reading.

### A textual support Draper does not cite
His argument that light and truth are conditioned on obedience has a stronger proof text than the ones he uses. **D&C 93:39**, in the same passage as his opening puzzle, states it outright:

> "That wicked one cometh and taketh away light and truth, **through disobedience**, from the children of men."

And **93:37**: "Light and truth forsake that evil one." The reciprocal is 93:40, "bring up your children in light and truth" — which is the commandment behind the rebuke to Frederick G. Williams (93:42) that opened the talk. Any presentation of this material should close that loop; Draper opens with verse 42 and never returns to it.

### The rhetorical function of the Laundromat
The talk's structure is confessional: a scholar admits, twice, that he could not understand a passage. The resolution arrives not in a study or a temple but in a **Laundromat in Tempe, Arizona**, while waiting for clothes to dry.

This is not incidental charm. It performs the doctrine. If light is capacity granted by grace rather than a reward for scholarly effort, then it should be expected to arrive unearned, in an undignified place, to a man who had already given up. The setting is the argument.

The companion move is the joke about light being a "wavicle" — self-deprecation that lowers the barrier before a genuinely abstract lecture. Draper knows he is about to be difficult and buys goodwill first.

## A Seam Worth Naming

There is an ordering tension between two of Draper's own statements, and an honest presentation should name it rather than paper over it.

**In section 6** he writes: "But first comes obedience to the word, then light, and finally truth." Obedience *precedes* light.

**In the summary** he writes: "Grace comes to all men freely… Grace allows light to flow unto each person. Thus light, through grace, is freely manifest unto all." Light is *freely manifest to all*, prior to any obedience — and agency consists in accepting or rejecting it.

These are reconcilable, and the reconciliation is worth teaching:

- Light is **offered** to everyone, freely, prior to obedience — this is grace (cf. D&C 84:46, "the Spirit giveth light to every man that cometh into the world").
- Light is **retained and increased** through obedience — cf. D&C 50:24, "he that receiveth light, **and continueth in God**, receiveth more light."

So obedience is not the price of admission; it is the condition of *continuance*. The first portion is given. Every portion after that is a response. Draper's two statements describe different stages of the same process, and saying so explicitly makes the talk stronger rather than weaker.

## The Architecture, Assembled

```
GRACE          universal, unconditional, not subject to agency
  ↓            "grace allows light to flow unto each person"
LIGHT          living, capacitating energy — actual celestial substance
  ↓            ← AGENCY OPERATES HERE: accept or reject (D&C 93:31–32)
CAPACITY       the enlarged container (the pint becomes a quart)
  ↓            retained and increased by obedience (D&C 50:24)
TRUTH          knowledge of things as they are, were, and are to come
  ↓
GLORY          "glorified in truth" (D&C 93:28)
```

And running alongside it, the reciprocal loop that drives the whole ascent:

```
receive grace → extend grace → receive more grace → ... → a fulness
        ("grace for grace" → "from grace to grace")
```

## Literary Devices

- **Confessional framing** — "I still don't understand what they are saying." A scholar's admitted defeat, twice, before any argument begins.
- **Bathos as doctrine** — the Laundromat. The mundane setting enacts the claim that light is unearned.
- **Self-deprecating technical humor** — the "wavicle."
- **Analogy as definition** — the pint and the quart, which does more explanatory work than any of the abstract passages.
- **Lexical archaeology** — arguing from Webster's 1828 to recover a reading lost to modern usage.
- **Explicit signposting** — "To rephrase for emphasis," "For emphasis, let me say again," "To sum up this portion." Draper repeatedly flags that he knows the material is hard.
- **Chain-linking** — word = truth = light = Spirit (D&C 84:45), presented as a welded identity rather than a set of analogies.

## Teaching Applications

| Audience | Entry point |
|---|---|
| **Sunday School / Come, Follow Me (D&C 93)** | The pint-and-quart analogy, then D&C 93:36 as a definition rather than a slogan. |
| **Youth / seminary** | Grace is not something you earn or even accept — it's already there. What you choose is whether to take the light it carries. Concrete and freeing. |
| **Those discouraged by study** | Light is capacity, not information. If the gospel feels too big to grasp, that is a container problem, and God has undertaken to enlarge the container. |
| **Parents** | D&C 93:40, "bring up your children in light and truth" — and Draper's opening question, *how do I teach my children light?* Answer: by extending grace, which is how light flows. |
| **Adult doctrine class** | The two definitions of intelligence (93:29–30), and the claim that Christ Himself progressed by grace. |
| **Alongside D&C 88** | Draper's opening puzzle is D&C 88:6–7, and he leans on 88:11–13, 66, 67 throughout. This talk is the natural companion to a study of section 88 — it answers the question section 88 raises but never defines: *what is light?* |

## The Governing Thesis

Draper takes three words that ordinary devotional usage treats as near-synonyms for *goodness* — light, truth, grace — and demonstrates that they are three distinct things standing in a fixed causal order.

**Grace is the disposition. Light is the capacity it delivers. Truth is what the capacity can then hold. And glory is what holding it makes you.**

The practical consequence is the talk's quiet gift: you are not asked to generate any of this. You are asked to accept the first link and pass it on. The rest compounds.
